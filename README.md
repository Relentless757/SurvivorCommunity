# Survivor Community (SurvivorCommunity) - 7 Days To Die 3.2

A State-of-Decay-style community on top of the NPC mods: survivors you recruit **live in your land claim**,
have **skills and traits**, share a **morale** that reflects how well the base is supplied and housed, eat
from a **stockpile of resource bundles**, and can be sent out or taken along.

**Requires** `0-SCore` and `0-XNPCCore` (the mod stays inert, with an error in the log, when either is
missing). Works with any NPC character pack built on the XNPCCore templates. Optional: `LandClaimUpgrade`
(the community then lives in the upgraded claim zone instead of the plain 41x41 square).
Harmony mod: EAC must be off. **Single player / host only in this version** - a remote player sees the
NPCs but cannot manage a community (v2).

## Milestone 1 - what works now

- **Recruiting.** Talk to any hireable NPC. Next to *"I am interested in hiring you"* there is now
  *"I could use someone like you in my community."* The survivor asks a **favour** first, rolled once per
  NPC: either **secure the nearest POI** (a journal quest with sleeper markers - no rally point, so nothing
  built there is ever reset; once the building is clear the quest's second step is **Return to <name>**
  and the marker moves to the survivor, who waits where you left them) or **bring supplies** (e.g. 15 food
  items, 5 medical items or 60 rounds). When the favour is done, the join option appears and they join
  **for free** - no dukes. Nobody is asked before you have a community home. A favour only completes through
  itself: clearing the POI without its quest does nothing, but if the building is already clear when you say
  *"Mark it"*, the survivor answers and you can say *"Actually, I've already cleared that."*. For supplies,
  *"Sure, I'll see what I can find."* puts a quest in the journal (marker on the survivor), and *"Here are the
  supplies"* is offered from then on - or straight away, if you already carry them when they ask.
- **Trust.** A survivor who is not in your community is Neutral toward you, Friendly once their favour is done,
  and Hostile for good the moment your damage lands on them - any hit, melee, shot, thrown weapon or explosion.
  A hostile survivor attacks you on sight, has no talk prompt for you, drops any favour they offered, won't be
  hired or recruited by you (one hired or recruited anyway - another player's doing - drops the grudge), and counts
  as an enemy for your turrets and for clearing a POI. Running them over counts; your turrets and traps don't, nor
  does a molotov's fire. Your own people (unless `CommunityFriendlyFire`) and hired companions can't be hurt by you (so a
  stray shot can't turn them), and traders keep their protection. `sc trust <id|name>` shows
  where one stands; `sc trust <id|name> forgive` clears a grudge (testing).
- **Snapshot.** While you talk to a survivor, a card on the left of the screen shows who they are: name and role,
  portrait, health, stamina (from Cardio - NPCs have no stamina stat), mood (a face and a word), traits and the four
  skills plus their specialty as stars. Someone not in your community gets their traits, skills and mood rolled the
  first time you talk to them, kept on the body, and brings exactly those when they join (`ScProfile`); their
  traits read "????" until their favour is done. The body is kept once you ask about their favour; one you only
  chatted with goes with the chunk like any wild survivor. A member who leaves keeps their record as it is then
  (traits by name). In multiplayer the host rolls profiles, so another player sees a stranger's card once the host
  has talked to them. The layout is generated by `Tools/generate_snapshot_ui.py`; the mood
  faces are `scui_mood_*` in the mod's UIAtlas. "Personal morale" is called "Mood" everywhere now.
- **Home.** The community lives in your land claim. The first land claim you own becomes home
  automatically; the land claim block's radial menu gets **Set as community home** and **Community**.
- **Walking.** The survivors (and the raiders) are 0-XNPCCore's player-body NPCs, and 0-XNPCCore's own controller
  drives their walking and running and the animator's locomotion. The fixes this mod once needed for its own
  player-body classes (a move helper that would not start a non-root-motion body, a path direction overwritten by
  the body's facing, an animator fed a tenth of the real speed) went with those classes; see "Survivors with the
  player's body" for the few body fixes that remain.
- **Friendly fire.** The NPC framework shields every friendly NPC from the player twice over - as allies while
  they follow you, and by faction while they do not - so shots at any of them passed through without a mark.
  The mod turns that into the division a community needs: an NPC who is not one of your people can be killed,
  and your own members cannot, unless you set `CommunityFriendlyFire` true in the config block. Traders stay
  immune either way, NPCs still cannot shoot each other, and zombies were never shielded.
- **Staying home.** Residents hold a Guard order, and the NPC framework's combat actions will chase a target
  as far as it runs, so the resident package adds a leash: more than 25 m from their post (with zone roaming:
  and outside the home zone) they walk back
  instead of chasing (they still defend themselves at arm's length). Followers standing next to you hold
  position rather than letting the framework's wander behaviour walk them off.
- **Residents.** A recruit who joins inside the base is stationed at a post near the land claim block;
  one who joins elsewhere follows you until you walk into the base. By day residents walk about the whole
  base (`ResidentRoamZone`: a stop anywhere in the home zone, `ResidentRoamPauseMin`..`Max` seconds there,
  then the next); at night, or with an enemy within 30 m, they go back to their post, stand quiet, fight
  whatever comes close and walk back. They are not in your party: no teleporting after you, no hired-NPC
  HUD entry. They survive save/reload and chunk unloads. While any player is within `HomeKeepLoadedRange`
  (250 m) of the base the whole home zone stays loaded (`HomeKeepLoaded`), so a survivor at the edge of your
  view distance is not unloaded and rebuilt every time you cross a chunk line.
  A resident that gets wedged in furniture or a doorway (no progress for `StuckSeconds`, or far from the
  post for long - with zone roaming, outside the home zone for long) is moved back to their post; the roam
  task also gives up on a blocked target. While it is
  fighting (a live enemy within 30 m) it gets three times `StuckSeconds` first, so a fight is not mistaken for
  a wedge. A post is always a spot with a real floor - a full block or terrain, preferably in the open, never
  the top of furniture, a plate, a ramp or a railing (roams aim the same way) - and a post from an older save
  that is not one is replaced the first time it loads (the log names what it stood on). A resident stuck at its
  post itself, or moved back three times within three minutes, gets a new post rather than another teleport;
  with no other spot to give, the check waits twice as long each time. Nothing counts while the game is paused,
  and a resident standing within a step of its post just stands there. `sc repost <member>` gives a new post by
  hand.
- **Defending the base.** Residents go after any awake enemy that gets inside the home zone within
  `DefendBaseRadius` (22 m) of their post (of themselves, while they walk the base with `ResidentRoamZone`), also
  one they cannot see from there - a zombie in the next room,
  on another floor, behind a wall. They walk to it until they can hit it (with a gun: in range and a clear
  shot), fight it, and walk back to their post. With a gun, when you are in their line of fire they stand
  still facing the enemy until you step aside, rather than closing in on your fight; when another member (or
  any friendly NPC) is in the line they close in to about 6.5 m, which usually opens the line, then wait.
  Within about 4.5 m of the enemy your people hold their fire while you or another friendly NPC is in the
  line, whatever they were doing (the NPC framework skips its sight check at that range). That includes your
  followers: in a close fight with you in front of them they aim and wait rather than fire past you, and swap
  to their melee weapon only once the zombie gets round you or hits them. Farther out the framework
  re-checks the line five times a second, so a shot fired just as you step in can still come; it hits you
  for nothing (friendly NPCs cannot hurt you) but is a wasted round. A deer or a dead body in the line does
  not stop them. A path that
  would take them more than 23 m from their post (with `ResidentRoamZone`: more than 6 m outside the home zone)
  (a long detour round a wall to a door) makes them give up
  for 20 s and walk back, instead of trading places with the 25 m leash. Sleepers in a base built in a POI
  are left asleep, Cowardly members stay put, and followers, runners and outpost stand-ins are not affected.
  An enemy they cannot reach holds them at most three times `StuckSeconds`; they are then moved back to
  their post and leave unseen enemies alone for a minute (what they see they still fight) - only after a
  move made during a fight; a resident freed from a plain wedge goes on defending at once.
  `DefendBaseRadius` 0 turns it off (they fight only what they see, as before); at most 23, inside the
  25 m leash. They only notice enemies within about 30 m of themselves (the NPC framework's search box),
  which covers the whole radius while they are near their post.
- **Followers.** From the roster (or the SCore companion window once they follow) a member can follow
  you like a normal hire - at most `MaxFollowers` (2) at a time - and be sent home again.
- **Skills and traits (data).** Each survivor rolls 2 traits, starting stars in Cardio / Wits / Fighting /
  Shooting and a specialty (Medicine, Cooking, Mechanics, Gardening, Chemistry, Craftsmanship). Kills
  already earn Fighting / Shooting XP. Gameplay effects come with milestone 3.
- **Persistence.** `<save>/survivor_community.txt` is the source of truth (atomic write, `.bak`).
- **Console:** `sc status | members | day | morale <n> | give <res> <n> | facility | drinks | relink | repost <member> | home | dump | save`.

## Milestone 2 - packing station, stockpile, daily needs

- **Packing Station** (`scPackingStation`, workbench recipe: 30 wood, 5 forged iron, 2 duct tape). It is
  a lockable crate with an 8x4 box. Open it and a strip appears under the grid: **Pack** food / water /
  medical / ammo / materials / fuel / luxuries turns what is in the box into **bundle units** (points = the
  items' economic value, `PointsPerBundle<Resource>` per unit; the most valuable stacks go first and anything
  short of a whole unit stays in the box), handed out biggest first (see *Water, luxuries and bundle sizes*),
  **Deposit bundles** moves every bundle in the box into the community **stockpile** at its size, and **Take**
  puts one Small bundle back in the box. The strip only shows on a station.
- **Bundles** (`scBundle<Res>Small/Medium/Large`) are plain items: they cannot be opened, sold or
  scrapped back into goods. Luxury = the `LuxuryItems` list (alcohol), water = a `drinks` item that hydrates,
  food = anything else tagged `food`/`drinks`, medical = `medical`, ammo = `ammo`, fuel = gasoline (the
  `gasoline` tag or the `FuelItems` list), materials = the `Resources` group or `resource*` items (wood and coal
  are materials even though they burn; empty jars too).
- **Bundles in the world** (`Config/loot.xml`): a small chance in the containers they belong in - food
  piles and cupboards; medical piles, medicine cabinets, medical coolers and Pop-N-Pills crates; ammo
  piles, military lockers, Shotgun Messiah crates and ammo displays; Pass-N-Gas crates (fuel);
  construction crates and toolboxes (materials); liquor piles and beer coolers (luxuries) - a pile drops the
  bundle of its own size - the five classic kinds in air drops (Medium), and as trader quest rewards (food /
  medical / ammo: a Small from tier 1, Medium from tier 2, Large at tier 3; resource rewards offer a materials
  or fuel bundle). Pop-N-Pills crates carry medical bundles and Shamway crates food and water bundles more often
  than anything else (Small 15% + Medium 5%; water 12% + 4%) - the only place water bundles are found.
- **Daily needs.** At each day rollover the community eats `FoodPerMemberDay` (0.5) food bundles per
  member (trait multipliers apply, fractions carry over) and uses one medical bundle per injured member
  (which heals them). This happens whether or not the base is loaded. **Ammo has no daily upkeep:** it is
  spent only when a survivor's gun is refilled in the base or at an outpost (see *Ammo* under the survivors
  below), and the day's report counts the ammo bundles that took. A tooltip and the ledger (`sc status`) report
  what was used, whether anyone went hungry, and whether a survivor ran low while the stockpile had no ammo; the
  hungry flag feeds morale in milestone 4.

## Water, luxuries and bundle sizes (round wl)

- **Bundle sizes.** Every resource has a **Small, Medium and Large** bundle worth **1, 2 and 3** stockpile units.
  Packing makes Large first, then one Medium or Small for the rest; Take gives a Small. The old one-size bundles
  still deposit as one unit; nothing makes or drops them any more. Icons: the matching vanilla piles (food, ammo,
  liquor small/medium/large; meds pile A small, B medium and large; cinder blocks 03/01/02; fuel and water a single
  barrel small, the four-barrel set medium and large).
- **Water** (`Resource.Water`): any `drinks` item that hydrates (a `$waterAmountAdd` or water-stat effect above 0)
  and is not alcohol - river and boiled water, tea, coffee, juice, Mega Crush, smoothies. Stews and canned soups
  hydrate too but are tagged `food` and stay food. Members drink `WaterPerMemberDay` (1) a day; short of it the
  community is **thirsty**, the hunger rules again on top of hunger (morale -20 - a water outpost's cistern floors
  it at `OutpostCisternFloor` -10 - every mood -10, `buffSCThirsty` -10% run speed and damage). Water bundles
  are loot only in Shamway crates.
- **Where water comes from:** packing drinks; Shamway crates; **supply runs** for water (the dialog and the
  window's run picker; water outposts' POIs first; a **gardener** is the water specialist); the **radio barter**
  (water can be given or asked for; luxuries cannot); **water outposts** (row `waterworks`: the Water Works, the water
  towers - tier 0 and allowed anyway through `OutpostAnyTierRows` - and modded wells, cisterns, aqueducts,
  watering holes; `OutpostBase` water 1.5 a day at tier 2; a scavenge outpost may fetch water when it runs
  lowest); and **facilities inside the home zone**, counted with the base's scan:
  - **wells** (`V3-OakravenWaterFillers`): the five models are levels 1-5 (`WellBlocks`), `WellWaterPerLevel`
    (2) water a day per level - 2/4/6/8/10. The well kit places Level 1 only (`WellsPlaceLevelOne`).
  - **dew collectors** (vanilla, `DewCollectorBlocks`): `DewWaterPerLevel` (1) a day per level - 1..5. Their
    level lives in the registry by position (picking one up forgets it). They keep filling jars as before.
- **Upgrading a facility:** hold E on it inside the community: **Check** (first, so a tap only reads it: level,
  water a day, next cost) and **Upgrade** (stockpile materials, `FacilityUpgradeCost` 4/8/12/16 for levels 2-5).
  A well becomes its next model; a dew collector's level goes up. These hooks are Harmony patches by block type
  (`ScFacilities`): this mod loads before the wells mod, so its XML cannot reach those blocks.
- **Luxuries** (`Resource.Luxury`): alcohol - the `LuxuryItems` list (beer and Grandpa's moonshine, Awesome Sauce
  and Learning Elixir); never food or water. Found in liquor piles and beer coolers. Not used up by the day: they
  are spent on **Activities**.
- **Activities** - a third tab in the community window. **Prepare a round of drinks**: `DrinksLuxury` (4)
  luxuries + `DrinksFood` (1) food for +`DrinksMorale` (15) morale over `DrinksMinutes` (15) **real** minutes
  (counted only while the game runs unpaused, saved with the registry), once per game day. Meant for the days
  after a death.
- **Window and HUD:** the resources strip shows all seven (water with its days left or "+N a day from the wells");
  the Home Status panel shows water between food and meds; morale lists Water and the round of drinks.
- **Console:** `sc give water|luxury <n>`, `sc run <member> water`, `sc barter water <res>`, `sc facility` (the wells and dew collectors in the loaded home zone,
  their levels and water), `sc facility level <x y z> <n>`, `sc drinks [on|off]`.

## Milestone 3 - skills and traits that do something

- **Core skills** (1-7 stars each). Fighting adds melee damage and Shooting adds ranged damage
  (`MeleePerStar` / `RangedPerStar`, 10% per star above the first); Cardio adds run speed and max health
  (`SpeedPerStar` 4%, `HealthPerStar` 8%). The bonuses ride on the NPC as `buffSCSkills`, which reads the
  mod's cvars, so they survive reloads and show in `sc members`. XP: kills (Fighting or Shooting by the
  weapon held), time spent following you (Cardio, one point per `FollowXpSeconds`), every day lived
  (Wits, `DailyWitsXp`).
- **Specialties** work at the day rollover, for stationed members only. The best **cook** cuts food use
  (`CookFoodPerStar`, floor 50%). **Gardeners** turn living crops inside the base into food bundles
  (`CropFoodPerStar` x stars per crop per day). A **medic** stretches medical bundles (one bundle heals
  1 + floor(stars x `MedicPerStar`) injuries). **Craftsmen** lower the packing threshold at the station
  (`PackBonusPerStar`, floor 60%). Mechanics and Chemistry pay off on supply runs (milestone 5). Doing the
  job earns specialty XP.
- **Traits** now show on the NPC as buffs with a description, food-use multipliers apply, Quick Study
  speeds XP, and **Cowardly** survivors flee instead of fighting (XNPCCore's own Flee action).
- **Hungry / Injured** are real: a community out of food makes every member 10% slower and weaker,
  an untreated injury 15-20%, until the next rollover with supplies.
- **Base scan**: beds, facilities and crops inside the whole claim zone feed the specialties and morale.
  They are counted from each loaded chunk's list of model blocks, so a count is cheap: every 15 s, every
  second while the community window is open, at each new day and with `sc scan`. A chunk that is not
  loaded keeps its last count (saved in the registry), so leaving the base does not lose beds.
  **Beds** are every sleeping-bag class block (bedrolls, king beds, bunk beds, and bed mods that use
  that class) with `BedsBySleepingBag`, plus the `BedBlocks` name prefixes (bare-frame beds, mattresses,
  hospital beds), minus `BedBlocksExclude` (a bunk frame with no mattress, a rolled-up mattress).
  Facilities match `FacilityBlocks` by name; crops are model-shaped `planted*` blocks (the flat flower
  crops are not food and do not count). `ScanRadius` above 0 limits the count to that many blocks
  around the land claim block.
- **Console:** `sc xp <id|name> <cardio|wits|fighting|shooting|spec> <n>`, `sc injure <id|name>`, `sc scan`.

## Milestone 4 - morale and the community window

- **Morale** (0-100) is recomputed from the registry: 50, plus days of food in the stockpile (or -20 when
  hungry), medicine (+5, or -10 with an untreated injury and no bundles), beds against members (+10, or
  -5 per missing bed), facilities (+3 each, campfire / workbench / forge / chemistry station / cement
  mixer, up to +15), +2 each for ammo, materials and fuel in stock, trait offsets, and recent deaths
  (`DeathPenalty` 15 each, fading `DeathDecayPerDay` 3 per day). Each survivor's **mood** is morale plus
  their trait offset, minus 15 while injured and 10 while the community is hungry. The enclaves add parts of their own
  (Allies, Comfort in Numbers, Enclave lost: see **Allies (E6)**), and so do infestations near home (see **Infestations
  (I1)**, *Near home*).
- **Consequences.** Above `HighMoraleThreshold` (75) everyone earns XP half again as fast. Below
  `LowMoraleThreshold` (25) for `LowMoraleDays` (2) days in a row the unhappiest survivor threatens to
  leave (tooltip, ledger, window flag) and leaves at the next rollover unless morale is back above the
  line. A survivor whose mood is under 25 refuses to follow you.
- **HUD.** A morale icon with the number sits in your buff bar while the community has members.
- **The community window** opens from the ESC menu (**Community**, under SCore's entries), the land
  claim block's radial **Community**, and a member's dialog (*Let's talk about the community*). Left:
  one row per survivor (status, mood, skills, specialty; injured rows are red) with paging; morale
  breakdown, recent days and the stockpile under it. Right: the selected survivor's skills, specialty,
  traits and flags, and the orders **Follow me** / **Go home** / **Ask to leave** (press twice).
- **Member dialog orders.** Talking to a member also offers *Come with me*, *Go back home* and *It is
  time for you to leave the community* (with a confirmation line).
- **Console:** `sc morale` prints the breakdown; `sc morale <n>` is a session-only override (-1 clears).

## Milestone 5 - supply runs

- **Send a survivor out** from the community window (five buttons under the orders, one per resource)
  or their dialog (*I need you to go find supplies* > what to look for). Only a stationed, uninjured
  survivor with mood 25 or better goes, at most `MaxRunners` (3) at once. They vanish from the base
  (hidden and parked in place, not teleported) and are back at the next day rollover after `RunDays` (1).
- **What they bring back**: `RunYieldBase` (2) bundles, +15% per Wits star above the first, +15% per
  specialty star for the matching job (Mechanics for materials and fuel, Chemistry for medicine, Cooking
  for food), trait multipliers (Tinkerer, Hoarder), and x1.25 when morale is above 75. Straight into the
  stockpile before that day's needs are taken.
- **Risk**: `RunRiskBase` (15%) minus 2% per Fighting and Shooting star above the first. A bad roll means
  they come back injured with half the haul, or - a quarter of bad rolls - never come back: a death like
  any other (morale penalty, deaths list); their hidden entity is removed when its chunk next loads.
- Runs earn Wits XP (`RunWitsXp`) and specialty XP for the matching job. A runner keeps eating from the
  stockpile but is not counted as a guard for ammo, a cook, a medic or a gardener while away.
- **Console:** `sc run <id|name> <food|meds|ammo|mats|fuel>`; `sc day` resolves runs at the rollover.

### Physical runs (`PhysicalRuns`, on by default)

The runner no longer vanishes. They **walk out of the base** to a real POI that has what they were sent for, and
walk back.
- **Destination.** The POI is `RunMinDist`-`RunMaxDist` (150-700) m from home. The first choice is a site the
  radio scouted for that resource. Next, a POI whose type produces it, primary producers and nearer sites first.
  Last resort: any scavenging site. Never an outpost, and never a POI where another runner, radio contact or
  favour already is.
- **The trip.** They run to the site, spend `RunSiteHours` (6) game hours walking round its ground floor, then
  run home and walk the last stretch to their post. Out of range they cover the distance at `RunWalkMps` (4 m/s,
  a little under their real running pace to allow for detours).
- **In the world while you are near.** Within your view distance they are a real NPC you can follow, escort, talk
  to (*Head home now.*) or watch fight. Further out, the mod captures the NPC into a snapshot kept in the
  registry: the same data the game uses to park vehicles and drones (name, health, buffs, bag, weapon, pack). It
  removes the NPC and walks them on along the route on the game clock. When you come near where they are by then,
  the same NPC (same entity id) is rebuilt there, out of your line of sight.
- **Only one copy, ever.** A runner is never saved in a chunk; the registry is their only copy. A chunk unload
  (teleport, bed respawn) captures them first. Quitting takes every runner out of the world before the save. Any
  old copy a chunk still holds is removed on load.
- **Risk.** The run's dice are rolled only while the runner is out of your sight. Escort them and real zombies
  decide instead.
- **The haul** counts the moment they reach the edge of the base, then they walk in to their post.
- **Distress calls** are rolled when they reach the site: they themself are pinned there (no stand-in), and the
  zombies go for them. Help them and they come home with the rescue bonus. Leave them for `RunDistressHours`
  and it ends the way bad runs do. Walking away mid-fight resets the fight.
- **Recall** (the Follow button in the window, or *Head home now.* in their dialog) turns them round. A search cut
  short brings a share of the find.
- **Fallback.** With no POI in range, `PhysicalRuns=false`, or on a dedicated server, the old hidden run above is
  used. Runs saved before this version finish the old way too.
- **Console:** `sc runners` lists each runner: leg, in world or not, position, distance, snapshot size, measured
  speed. `sc run <name> skip|offline|recall|home` drives a run for testing.

## Doors

- Members open closed doors on their way (roaming, following, going home, supply runs) and shut them again
  behind them once they are through and nobody is in or right beside the doorway. Double doors and gates work from
  either leaf.
- **Locked doors.** A member opens a locked door only if it is your lock (you own it or are on its list), and
  `MembersOpenOwnerLockedDoors` is true (the default). A locked door in a POI, or anyone else's, stays shut. Set it to
  false and your locked doors keep your own people out too.
- **Drawbridges and hatches** are opened too, and shut (the bridge raised) behind them, so a base whose only way
  out is a hatch or a bridge doesn't trap its people. Set `MembersOpenHatches` to false to keep members off them.
- **Raiders** open unlocked doors (SCore 3.2.36 does that for every NPC); locked doors stop them.
- Why this was needed: SCore's own door opener only runs from some of its tasks (and did nothing on 3.x doors
  before SCore 3.2.36), and the game's opener (which bandits use) is off for SCore NPCs, while the pathfinder still
  routes them through closed doors. Members used to walk into a shut door and stand there until they were teleported
  home.

## Linked storage

- **One container for every box in the base.** The land claim block's radial gets **Linked storage**
  (also a button in the community window). It opens in the normal loot window - left panel, backpack
  beside it, drag and drop, shift-click both ways, the transfer buttons - showing every stack in every
  player-placed storage box inside the zone, sorted by name, as one grid. With Grid Inventory the grid
  scrolls: every stack, up to about 240 at once (Grid Inventory's loot window has 260 item widgets and
  slot N can only be drawn by widget N), then twelve empty rows to drop into. The boxes' whole free room
  is in the strip's header, however many boxes there are; Refresh lays the view out again once the empty
  rows are used. On vanilla's loot grid it shows 10 rows. The strip under the grid shows the box count,
  stacks and free room, how many stacks did not fit, and the Refresh button. `LinkedStorageRows` in the
  config can lower the row limit.
- **It is a real view, not a copy.** Taking, half-taking, swapping or merging in a cell happens in the
  real box slot behind it, so the box on the wall changes with it. Anything dropped on an empty cell, or
  shift-clicked from the backpack, goes into the **first box with room for it**. If no box has room the
  item comes back to you. With Grid Inventory and its `zzzz_GridInv_SurvivorCommunity` patch, room means
  cells: a box only takes an item its own grid could seat, the linked view is sized in cells, and the
  strip counts free cells - a box with a free slot but no run of cells for a rifle does not
  count.
- Boxes in chunks that are not loaded are out of reach (the strip says how many chunks were skipped).
  The packing station is not part of it. Host only.

## Radio station

- **The block.** `scRadioStation` (workbench: forged iron, electric and mechanical parts, polymers) is a
  powered consumer - wire it like a light. **Use radio** opens its window while it has power.
- **Air drops on request.** With this mod installed vanilla's scheduled air drops never come; the radio
  requests one, and the sandbox **air drop frequency** is its cooldown - the same day range vanilla would
  have scheduled (every 3 days, 1-7 days...). Turn that setting off and the radio cannot call a plane.
  `RadioOnlyAirdrops=false` restores vanilla's own drops.
- **Call for survivors.** One call every `RadioCallDays`: one of our player-body survivors (never a pack NPC,
  see *No new pack NPCs* below) answers from a real POI
  `RadioContactMinDist`-`RadioContactMaxDist` metres from home, marked on the map. They exist as a record
  (not an entity) until you get near: the first time that chunk loads the NPC spawns there, standing in
  the building with a favour to ask like any recruit. Unvisited contacts fade after `RadioContactDays`.
- **Distress calls.** While a radio is powered, a survivor sent on a supply run may radio for help
  (`RadioDistressChance`): a quest marks the building they are pinned in. Get within `RescueTriggerRange`
  and the survivor appears there with zombies closing in - `DistressZombiesBase` plus your gamestage, but
  never fewer than `DistressZombiesPerCombatStar` per combat star they have, so a veteran calling for
  help means real trouble. Clear them and the run comes home safe with `RescueBonus` on the haul; ignore
  the call and the run ends the way bad runs do. If the survivor falls out there, they are dead.
- The radio is also what outposts report in to (see **Outposts**).
- **Enclaves (E6).** The window's right-hand column lists the enclaves you know, their open requests and a siege call
  with *On our way* / *We can't come* (see **Allies (E6)**).
- **Allies (E7).** Its Allies panel trades supplies with an Allied enclave, once a day each (see **Gifts and ally barter
  (E7)**).

All five milestones, linked storage and the radio are in. See the plan's non-goals for what v1 deliberately leaves
out (multiplayer clients, a player-menu tab, portraits, NPC hunger, base building by survivors).

## Outposts

Hold cleared buildings away from home and they supply the community. Everything is registry bookkeeping, so it keeps
working with the outpost and the home both unloaded. The in-game checklist is `TESTING.md`.

**Establishing and daily production**
- **Establish.** Clear a building at least `OutpostMinDistance` (150 m) from home and place an ordinary land claim in
  it (or craft an **Outpost Flag**, which establishes itself when placed). Then pick **Establish outpost** on the
  claim. It needs the home radio powered and the fee (`OutpostFeeMats` + `OutpostFeeFuel` from the stockpile).
- **What it makes** depends on what the building is (`sc poi`, and the `OutpostRows` / `OutpostPrefabs` tables):
  groceries, diners and farms make food; clinics make medical; army sites, gun stores and police make ammo;
  hardware, industry and junkyards make materials; gas stations and refineries make fuel. A building that matches
  no row scavenges whatever you are shortest of. More outposts of the same kind give less each.
- **Every rollover** each outpost adds its yield to the stockpile. The yield is scaled by tier, level, the people
  posted there, what you built inside it (beds, facilities, crops), and its output mode (quiet / normal / push).
  - Upkeep costs materials; hauling costs fuel. Unpaid upkeep halves the yield (run-down); unpaid haulage cuts it
    to three quarters (on foot).
  - Without power at the home radio (or a powered **relay** radio inside the outpost), bundles wait there,
    at most `OutpostPendingDays` of yield, and arrive once it reports in.
- **Slots:** 1 + one per 4 members + one per 5 points of Leadership morale, at most `OutpostMax`. Outposts over the
  limit go idle; nothing is destroyed.

**Protection**
- Sleepers never respawn in the building, trader quests and rally resets pass it by, and the claim limit never
  switches off the home or an outpost.
- If its claim goes, the outpost is lost, with a morale penalty that fades.

**At the outpost** (the claim's menu)
- **Outpost** opens the community window on the Outposts tab.
- **Linked storage** radios home and opens the home storage right there.
- **Outpost storage** links that building's own boxes.
- The claim's box has the Pack / Deposit strip.
- **Disband outpost** needs two presses; the claim stays yours.

**People and growth**
- Post up to two members (a second post at level 2). They are hidden at home like runners, still eat, and add
  yield: more when their specialty fits.
- While you visit, their stand-ins guard the claim. If a stand-in dies there, that survivor died.
- Levels 2 and 3 are bought from the stockpile.
- Each kind of producing outpost gives a perk:
  - **canteen:** hunger hurts less;
  - **field clinic:** one free heal a day;
  - **armed escort:** safer runs;
  - **salvage yard:** cheaper fees;
  - **motor pool:** bigger runs.
- Posted crafters and chemists turn surplus into ammo and medical.

**Radio**
- **Scout** marks the nearest sites for a resource.
- **Barter** trades 3 bundles of one resource for 1 of another, once a day (food, medical, ammo, materials, fuel
  and water; not luxuries).
- It lists every outpost and answers raiders.
- Staffed outposts sometimes draw survivors in, and runs in distress sometimes hole up at an outpost.

**Risk** (`OutpostAttacks`, off by default)
- Each outpost has a **security** rating from its staff, tier, your visits, ammo, the defenses you built
  (spikes, wire, traps, turrets) and fights won.
- **Attack calls** give you `OutpostAttackHours` to get there and fight.
- Ignored attacks are settled on security:
  - **held;**
  - **damaged:** no yield for 2 days, or repair it for materials;
  - **overrun:** the sleepers are back, and the staff come home hurt. Nobody dies in a fight you did not attend.
- Blood moons roll every outpost you were not standing at.
- Neglected outposts get squatters.
- Past gamestage 30, raiders may demand supplies.
- Good-news caches (on by default) leave bundles in the claim's box.

**Console:** `sc poi [near r | classify prefab | census]`,
`sc outpost [list | yield | add | remove | post | recall | upgrade | mode | security | attack | demand | pay | refuse | repair ...]`,
`sc radio`, `sc scout <res>`, `sc barter <give> <get>`.

**SurvivorOrigins:** the fact `sc_outposts` and the events `sc_ev_outpost` (established) and `sc_ev_outpost_retaken` are
published for a future Leadership release; won outpost fights count as `sc_ev_rescue`.

## Enclaves at a glance

You aren't the only survivors (`Enclaves_Plan.md`, milestones E1-E6; E7 on from the ideas' DESIGN.md). In short:
- **On the map (E1):** groups holed up in buildings across the map, about 0.3 per km² and a quarter of them raiders.
  You find one by going near, a powered radio picks one up every few days, and an ally tells you of more. Their
  buildings are theirs: no zombies, no land claims, no trader quests there.
- **Their people (E2):** a leader and the rest of the group, 3-5 people (3-6 for raiders), made the first time you come
  near and kept as records from then on. They defend their building, and attacking them makes them hostile for good.
- **Trade, enlist, recruit (E3):** the leader trades from the group's own stock; the others come along for a couple of
  days or join your community - for less the better you stand with them.
- **Requests (E4, E5):** the leader asks for supplies, a gun, a generator, a missing person found, an infestation
  cleared, or help when the dead come over their walls. How you answer moves your **standing**: Neutral, Friendly from
  30, Allied from 70.
- **Allies (E6):** an Allied group does something for your community every day (its benefit), lifts morale and tells
  you about other groups. Losing a friend hurts morale, and a new group moves in a few days after any group is lost.
- **Gifts and ally barter (E7):** hand a leader a bundle for a little standing, once a day, up to Friendly; trade
  supplies with your allies over the radio, once a day each.
- **Infestations (I1)** are buildings the dead overran: enclaves ask you to clear them, an ally's Neighborhood Watch
  keeps new ones away, and a building lost to a siege becomes one.

The radio window's Enclaves column shows the groups you know, their requests, your allies' barter and a siege call;
`sc enclave` is the console for all of it. Everything is the host's, kept in `survivor_community.txt`. The sections
below go milestone by milestone.

## Enclaves (E1: on the map)

You aren't the only survivors. Other groups have holed up in buildings across the map: families, drifters, preppers
and raiders. Each group is an **enclave**, with a name and a building of its own. This first milestone puts them on
the map: you can find them, see them on the map, and their buildings are theirs. Their **people** are E2, the next
section; trade (E3), requests and sieges (E4, E5) and allies (E6) follow; the infestations are I1, below.

**When they appear**
- **A new save** gets its enclaves the moment it first starts. Nothing spawns: they are records until you get near.
- **An existing save** gets them the first time this build loads it (the *late seed*, `EnclaveLateSeed`). Then no
  enclave goes within `EnclaveClaimBuffer` (150 m) of anyone's land claim, none into the chunks around you, and
  buildings you have never visited are preferred, so nobody moves into one you already looted.
- **Every later load** finds each enclave's building again. If it is gone (a POI pack removed), the enclave moves to
  the nearest free building within `EnclaveRelocateRange` (1 km), keeping its name. If the free ones there are only
  loaded, near a claim or in use just then, it waits off the map for a later load; if there is none at all, it is
  dropped. Raising `EnclavesPerKm2` adds more on the next load; lowering it removes none.
- The same save always gets the same enclaves: everything is drawn from the world seed.

**How many, and where**
- About `EnclavesPerKm2` (0.3) per km², at least `EnclaveMin` (4), at most `EnclaveMax` (36): 11 on a 6k map, 20 on 8k.
  They are spread about 1 km apart.
- Tier 1-4 buildings that have zombies, 20-80 m across. Rural and residential buildings are likelier; downtown,
  caves, mines, bunkers, lots, rubble, bridges and the like are never picked (`EnclaveKindWeights`,
  `EnclavePoiExcludes`).
- At least 300 m from traders, spawn points and the map's edge, and never a building someone has claimed (a land
  claim, a bedroll, an outpost), your home zone, an infested building, or a building a radio contact, favour, supply
  run, radio scouting mark or one of your quests in progress uses.
- **Near your start:** up to 2 non-hostile enclaves 400-1500 m from where you spawn (from home, in an existing save),
  when buildings that fit stand there.
- **About a quarter are raiders** (hostile; fewer when too few fit), never within 1.2 km of the start or a spawn point
  (600 m on maps under 4 km).
  Military, police, prison, junkyard and factory buildings, tier 3-4, and the wasteland and burnt forest suit them.
- **Names** are generated from the biome, the building and a leader or family: "The Pinefield Survivors", "The
  Holdouts at the Diner", "Jody's Lot", "The Rabid Rats". No name repeats in a save.

**Finding them**
- Come within `EnclaveDiscoverRange` (150 m) of one: "You've found The X, holed up at Y (Neutral)." It goes on the map
  and in the ledger.
- A **powered radio** picks one up every `EnclaveRadioRevealDays` (3) days: the nearest unknown enclave within
  `EnclaveRadioRange` (2 km) of home (of you, without a home). It tells you where: "900 m north-east".
- An **ally** tells you of the enclave nearest to them that you don't know (E6, below).
- **Markers** (owner-only): a grey fortress for a neutral enclave, a red skull for raiders, labelled with the name and
  the standing ("The Pinefield Survivors (Neutral)"). Raiders also show on the compass within 300 m. Friendly (green)
  and Allied (blue, on the compass within 600 m) follow the standing (E2 keeps it; E4's requests earn it), and a
  group that turns on you goes red.

**Their building is theirs**
- **No zombies.** Every sleeper room in it is marked cleared for good, and a room the game creates later (when its
  chunk first loads) is cleared the moment you would trigger it.
- **No trader quests**, no rally reset, no vanilla respawn there: it counts as claimed, like an outpost.
- **No land claims:** a claim whose square would reach into the building is refused, and the tooltip names who lives
  there. **No outposts** either.
- **In multiplayer** the host seeds them, and the zombies, trader quests and outposts are kept out for everyone. The
  claim check runs on the machine that places the claim, so for now only the host's own claims are refused. Finding
  them, the markers and the radio are the host's. A dedicated server seeds and protects them, but nobody finds them
  there yet.
- SC's own picks (radio contacts, recruit favours, supply runs, radio scouting) never send anyone there.
- The loot is the building's own loot, and its containers are its people's (E2, below).

**Config** (`Config/blocks.xml`, commented there): the switch `Enclaves`; how many `EnclavesPerKm2`, `EnclaveMin`,
`EnclaveMax`; spacing `EnclaveSpacingMin`, `EnclaveSpacingFactor`, `EnclaveSpacingFloor`; raiders `EnclaveHostileShare`,
`EnclaveHostileMinStart`; the start ring `EnclaveStartRing`, `EnclaveStartRingMin`, `EnclaveStartRingMax`,
`EnclaveStartRingSpacing`; which buildings `EnclaveTierMin`/`Max`, `EnclaveSizeMin`/`Max`, `EnclaveTraderDistance`,
`EnclaveSpawnDistance`, `EnclaveEdgeDistance`, `EnclaveKindWeights`, `EnclavePoiExcludes`; existing saves
`EnclaveLateSeed`, `EnclaveClaimBuffer`, `EnclaveUnvisitedWeight`, `EnclaveRelocateRange`; finding them
`EnclaveDiscoverRange`, `EnclaveRadioRevealDays`, `EnclaveRadioRange`.

**Console:** `sc enclave [list]`, `sc enclave info <id>` (the record, its footprint, its sleeper rooms),
`sc enclave reveal <id|all>` / `hide <id|all>`, `sc enclave sim [late]` (the seeding on this world as if it had none,
nothing saved), `sc enclave names [n] [hostile]`, `sc enclave reseed confirm` (testing: drops every enclave and seeds
again with the late rules). `sc poi` adds a line when the building you stand in is an enclave's.

**Saving:** the enclaves are `[enclave]` sections in `survivor_community.txt`, plus `enclaveid`, `enclaveseed` and
`enclaveradio` in `[community]`. This is **forward-only**: an older SurvivorCommunity build skips the `[enclave]`
sections as unreadable (a "degraded" load), ignores the three keys, and its next save drops them all.

**Switching them off:** set `Enclaves=false`. The records stay, but nothing is seeded, shown or protected, and the rooms
they kept clear (the world save remembers that) respawn after the loot respawn wait (never, when loot respawn is off).
**Before you uninstall SurvivorCommunity**, load the save once with `Enclaves=false`: otherwise those rooms stay empty
until something resets the building, such as a trader quest there.

## Enclave people (E2)

Every enclave now has people: a leader and the rest of the group, living in their building.

**Who they are, and how many**
- 3-5 people for a survivor enclave and 3-6 for raiders (`EnclaveGroupMin`/`Max`, `EnclaveRaiderGroupMin`/`Max`): the
  bigger the building, the more of them, give or take one.
- They are made the first time you come within `EnclaveSpawnRange` (150 m): SC's own wild survivors (raiders for a
  raider enclave) of the tier your gamestage gives then, so faces, sexes, weapons, skills and traits vary as wild
  spawns do. From then on each of them is a record: the same name, face, weapons, skills and traits on every visit.
  Like wild survivors they carry 0-XNPCCore's day-based levels (set again each visit); your members never do.
- The first one made is the **leader**. Each group has a **kind** from its building (`EnclaveKindRows`): growers at
  farms, medics at clinics, militia at military, police and gun stores, builders at industry and hardware stores, a
  road crew at gas stations and refineries, scavengers anywhere else.

**When they appear and go**
- Within `EnclaveSpawnRange` of the building, once its chunks are built for walking on, one a second, the leader
  first, and never popping up within `EnclaveNoPopRange` (50 m) in front of you. Your view distance decides when the
  chunks are built: at the default that is about 90-100 m out, so that is where they usually appear.
- When nobody is within `EnclaveSpawnRange` + `EnclaveDespawnMargin` (200 m) and nobody sees them, they go: their
  health and rounds are kept for next time. While they are out, the building's chunks stay loaded
  (`EnclaveHoldChunks`).
- They are never written into the world's save: a save and reload next to them brings the same people back from their
  records. Their bodies are SC's, so leaving and coming back never makes duplicates.
- Each day they heal, and survivors get the rounds they were made with back (`EnclaveDailyRestock`); a raider enclave's
  people start every visit with a fresh raider's stack anyway.

**What they do**
- Each has a **home spot** in the building (the places its author put sleepers first). By day the others walk about
  the building and its yard and stand a while; at night they idle near their spots. The **leader stays at their spot**
  all day, so you always know where to find them.
- They defend their building: an awake enemy on their grounds within `EnclaveDefendRadius` (22 m) of one of them is
  fought, seen or not. A chase never takes them more than `EnclaveLeashMargin` (8 m) past the building: they walk back.
- The cowardly ones run from a fight. Stuck ones are moved back to their spot, as residents are.

**Talking to them**
- The leader greets you by your **standing**: wary at Neutral, warm at Friendly, welcoming at Allied. Ask them
  *"Tell me about this place."* (their name, how many they are, the leader, the building).
- The others greet you the same way, and *"Where's your leader?"* tells you who leads and where they are from you.
- None of them can be hired, picked up or dismissed, by dialog or by voice; the leader's trade, enlisting and
  recruiting go through their own rows (E3, below), and the leader's requests through *"What do you need?"* (E4).

**Standing, warnings and hostility**
- Standing is 0 at first and goes from `EnclaveStandingMin` (-30) to 100: Neutral below 30, Friendly from 30, Allied
  from 70. The map marker follows it. E4's requests are how you earn it (and lose it), below.
- **Hitting one of them** is a warning: they shout *"Watch it!"* and the enclave's standing drops by
  `EnclaveWarnPenalty` (10). Hits within `EnclaveHitDebounce` (1.5 s) of each other are one attack (a shotgun blast).
  **Another hit within `EnclaveWarnWindow` (2 minutes), or killing one of them** (you, or a companion of yours), turns
  the **whole enclave hostile for good**. After the two minutes a hit is a fresh warning.
- **Threats:** ask the leader *"Hand over your supplies."* and confirm with *"I'm not asking."*: they hand over
  `EnclaveThreatBundlesMin`-`Max` (2-4) medium bundles, mostly of what their kind lives on (growers food, medics
  medical, militia ammo, builders materials, a road crew fuel), into your bag (the rest at your feet). Then they are
  hostile for good, after `EnclaveThreatGrace` (10 s) so you can walk away from the talk. *"Forget it."* takes it back.
- **A hostile enclave** shoots you on sight, and you can't talk to them any more; its marker is red. Your residents,
  your followers and your turrets fight its people, and they fight them back. A hostile survivor with a gun closes in
  like a raider, and investigates noise, but never leaves its building for long. Nothing makes them friendly again.

**Raider enclaves** are raiders: they attack on sight, have nothing to say, and stay home - noise or a chase does not
pull them off their building. Hitting them changes nothing; they are hostile already.

**Deaths, the leader, a group wiped out**
- Deaths are real. Whoever dies (to zombies, raiders or you) is dead for good; their body is left with its loot. You
  hear of it when you are near, and the ledger notes it.
- When the leader dies, the strongest of the rest (then the longest in the group) leads from the leader's spot.
- When the last of them dies, the enclave is gone: its marker comes off, and its building is free again - claims,
  quests, infestations and its containers; its sleeper rooms respawn after the loot respawn wait. Wipe out raiders and
  you're told "You wiped out <name>." (While a group is still being made, the first ones dying does not end it: the
  rest still come, a new leader first.)

**Their containers are theirs:** opening a loot container in their building is refused while the enclave exists -
"That belongs to <name>." (`EnclaveContainerLock`). Containers you placed are yours as always. **Breaking a container
open is not covered** (a known limit).

**No wild NPCs on their grounds:** SC's and 0-XNPCCore's wild survivors and raiders never spawn within
`EnclaveWildSpawnMargin` (40 m) of a living enclave, so a stranger is never mistaken for one of them (zombies and
animals spawn as before). `sc enclave list` counts the spawns kept off. A spawn kept off counts as vanilla's "none"
for that spawn entry, so wild NPCs also thin out a little in the rest of the 80 m spawn area round the enclave until
the entry resets.

**Config** (`Config/blocks.xml`, commented there): `EnclaveGroupMin`, `EnclaveGroupMax`, `EnclaveRaiderGroupMin`,
`EnclaveRaiderGroupMax`, `EnclaveSpawnRange`, `EnclaveDespawnMargin`, `EnclaveNoPopRange`, `EnclaveSpawnPerTick`,
`EnclaveHoldChunks`, `EnclaveDailyRestock`, `EnclaveLeashMargin`, `EnclaveDefendRadius`, `EnclaveStandingMin`,
`EnclaveWarnPenalty`, `EnclaveWarnWindow`, `EnclaveHitDebounce`, `EnclaveThreatBundlesMin`, `EnclaveThreatBundlesMax`,
`EnclaveThreatGrace`, `EnclaveContainerLock`, `EnclaveWildSpawnMargin`, `EnclaveKindRows`. With `Enclaves=false` every
body goes and none comes; the records stay.

**Console** (host): `sc enclave people <id>` (each person: role, name, class, state, health, spot, body),
`sc enclave info <id>` (adds the kind and the people), `sc enclave spawn <id>` (its people now, made first if need be,
when its chunks are loaded; no view rule), `sc enclave despawn <id>` (they go, and stay away 30 s),
`sc enclave standing <id> <points>` (greetings and the marker follow), `sc enclave hostile <id>` (no payout),
`sc enclave kill <id> [<person id>|all]` (the leader without one: deaths, succession and a group wiped out; `all` is
the whole group, those not made yet included - while one of theirs is missing on an E5 search and may be alive the
enclave stands, and a second `all` ends it).
`sc enclave reseed confirm` drops the people with the enclaves - the only way back from hostility.

**Saving:** the people are `[encperson]` sections in `survivor_community.txt` (each with the person's own fields, then
the same fields as a member), with `encpersonid` in `[community]` and `kind`, `turned` and `people` in each
`[enclave]`. Forward-only, as `[enclave]`: an older build drops them. A person whose enclave is missing is dropped.

**Limits:** host only - in multiplayer the host's world runs them; a dedicated server runs no SC ticker, so nobody
appears there. Breaking containers open is not stopped. A multiplayer client does not see the enclave dialog (only
"Nevermind").

## Trade, enlist and recruit (E3)

An enclave's people now deal with you: the leader trades, the others can come along for a couple of days or join your
community for good.

**Trade with the leader**
- The leader of an enclave that is not hostile is a real trader: *"Let's see what you have to trade."* (or Trade on
  their radial) opens vanilla's trade window. What they sell depends on the group's kind, from vanilla's own trader
  stock and smaller than a trader's: growers food and seeds, medics medicine, militia guns and ammo, builders tools and
  materials, a road crew fuel and vehicle parts, scavengers a bit of everything (`Config/traders.xml`).
- Each enclave keeps **its own stock**: what you buy is gone, what you sell them stays until the restock, which comes every 3 days
  the way vanilla traders do. It is kept when they despawn, when you reload, and when a new leader takes over.
- **Prices by standing:** at Neutral vanilla's both ways; at Friendly they pay you 20 % more for what you sell; at
  Allied they charge you 20 % less and pay 35 % more (`EnclaveBuy*` / `EnclaveSell*`). Your own barter perks count on
  top. A hostile enclave does not trade.

**Enlist one of them** (*"Can you come with me?"*, to anyone but the leader)
- They follow you and fight like a hired companion for 2 days (`EnclaveEnlistDays`), for 500 dukes at Neutral, 250
  at Friendly and nothing at Allied (`EnclaveEnlistCost*`), paid from your bag and toolbelt. There is no weekly Voice
  Control contract: SC handles it.
- One per enclave at a time, and they count toward your follower limit (`MaxFollowers`, 2) together with your own
  followers. When it can't be, the row is greyed with the reason: the dukes it needs, one of them already with you,
  or all the followers you can lead.
- They go home when you tell them (*"Go home now."*, or Voice Control's "dismiss"), when their days are up, when their
  enclave turns hostile (they leave you first), or when you go far enough that their body unloads (or you quit): they
  walk back to their spot when their building is near, and otherwise walk away and are home again the next time you
  visit. (SCore's companion window does not list them: it shows only its older body type.)
- If they die while with you, the enclave's standing drops by 5 (`EnclaveEnlistDeathPenalty`) and the ledger says so.

**Recruit one of them** (*"Would you join us?"*, to anyone but the leader, at home or with you)
- At **Neutral** they want their own favour first, as wild survivors do (a POI to clear or supplies to bring: SC's own
  favour statements and journal quest), and the join costs 10 standing (`EnclaveRecruitStanding`). At **Friendly** no
  favour, 10 standing. At **Allied** no favour and no cost.
- The enclave always keeps at least 2 people (`EnclaveRecruitMinLeft`), lets one go every 7 days
  (`EnclaveRecruitCooldownDays`), and not while its group is still being made; your community needs a home and room.
  The row is greyed with the reason when one of these is not met.
- They join with the same face, name, weapons, skills and traits you saw, and leave their enclave's records for good.
  The ledger notes who left whom.

**Config** (`Config/blocks.xml`, commented there): `EnclaveTraderIdBase` (the leader's trader ids in
`Config/traders.xml`: 200-205 - keep both in step), `EnclaveBuyNeutral`, `EnclaveBuyFriendly`, `EnclaveBuyAllied`,
`EnclaveSellNeutral`, `EnclaveSellFriendly`, `EnclaveSellAllied`, `EnclaveEnlistCostNeutral`,
`EnclaveEnlistCostFriendly`, `EnclaveEnlistCostAllied`, `EnclaveEnlistDays`, `EnclaveEnlistDeathPenalty`,
`EnclaveRecruitMinLeft`, `EnclaveRecruitCooldownDays`, `EnclaveRecruitStanding`.

**Console** (host): `sc enclave trade <id>` (the trader id, the leader, the stock and its last and next restock, the
prices now), `sc enclave release <id>` (their enlisted person goes home now), and `sc enclave people <id>` now shows
who is with you and until when, their favours, and when the next recruit can go.

**Saving:** each `[enclave]` gains `stock` (the leader's trade stock) and `recruited` (the day of the last recruit);
an enlisted person's `[encperson]` has `enlisted` (who, until when, which body) - a load always finds them home; an
enclave person's favour is a `[prospect]` with `encp` (whose). Forward-only, as E2's; an older build would also keep a
person saved while they were out with you (`state=enlisted`, a save from mid-enlistment) away from home for good.

**Limits:** host only, as E2. A multiplayer client sees neither the dialog nor the leader's Trade, and their trade
prices would be vanilla's. A stray hire entry can stay on the player after a crash while someone was enlisted (it is
cleared at the next load).

## Requests (E4)

An enclave's leader now asks you for help, and how you answer moves the enclave's standing: requests are how a group
becomes your friend and your ally, and how it stops being one.

**Standing**
- From `EnclaveStandingMin` (-30) to 100: Neutral below 30, Friendly from 30, Allied from 70. Hostile is separate:
  letting a group down never makes it hostile.
- **Gains:** a resource request +10 (`EnclaveGainResource`, whatever its size), a generator +15
  (`EnclaveGainGenerator`), a gun +3 to +30 by its tier and quality (below).
- **Losses** (Neutral / Friendly / Allied): saying no -5 / -5 / -12 (`EnclaveLossDecline`); agreeing, then giving up or
  running out of time -8 / -8 / -15 (`EnclaveLossFail`); leaving an offer unanswered until it lapses 0 / -3 / -15
  (`EnclaveLossLapse`). Keeping an ally is standing only: an Allied group let down is back to Friendly.
- The marker's colour, the leader's greeting and E3's prices follow the band as it changes; a tell names the new band
  ("The X is now Friendly.") and the ledger notes every request's end with the standing it moved.

**When they ask**
- At each day's rollover, every enclave you have found that is not hostile, has no open request and is past its
  cooldown may ask: 35 % at Neutral, 45 % at Friendly, 20 % at Allied (`EnclaveRequestChance*`). One per enclave, at
  most 3 open across the map (`EnclaveRequestMaxOpen`). After the day one of its requests closed, an enclave rests a
  whole day before it asks again (`EnclaveRequestCooldownDays`).
- What they ask (`EnclaveRequestKindWeights`): a resource most often, a gun, or - from day 10
  (`EnclaveGeneratorFromDay`) - a generator. A resource is one their kind doesn't live on (growers never ask for food,
  medics for medicine, militia for ammo, builders for materials, a road crew for fuel), small, medium or large: 1, 2
  or 3 bundle units (`EnclaveRequestSizeWeights`).
- With a **powered radio** you hear of it: *"The radio crackles: The X is asking for help."*, and the ledger says so.
  Without one you find out when you talk to their leader.
- An **offer stands for 3 days** (`EnclaveOfferLapseDays`, the day it's made counts): unanswered, it lapses. Days you
  miss (a long sleep, a long absence) still run their lapses and expiries in order; new requests come only on the
  newest day.

**Talking to the leader:** *"What do you need?"*, their first row.
- Nothing open: *"Nothing right now. We're managing."*
- **An offer:** they say what they need, how long you'll have once you agree, and how long they'll wait for your
  answer. Then:
  - *"Leave it with me."* - you agree. It's due by the end of the 4th day, counting today (`EnclaveDueDaysItems`). It
    goes in your journal as "Help The X" (what, to whom, by when) with a marker on their building. *"Thank you. We'll
    be here."*
  - *"I have it right here."* - only while you carry it: handed over on the spot.
  - *"Take one of these."* - a gun request, while you carry a gun: the gun pick (below).
  - *"No, I can't help with that."* - a failure now.
  - *"Let me think about it."* - the talk ends and nothing happens yet; the offer lapses when its days are up.
- **One you agreed to:** *"Still waiting on ..."* with the hand-over, the gun pick and *"I can't do it."* (a failure
  now).
- Every answer gets their reply, with the standing it moved. The journal quest can't be removed from the journal (tell
  the leader instead): it completes when you deliver and fails when you give up, run out of time, or the group turns
  hostile or is gone.

**What they take**
- **A resource:** packed bundles of that resource from your bag and toolbelt (the packing station makes them) - any
  sizes that add up. The bundles that overpay least (then the fewest) are taken; any overpay is a gift. Small 1,
  medium 2, large 3; the old one-size bundle counts 1.
- **A generator:** any item that places a generator - vanilla's generator bank, or another mod's.
- **A gun:** any firearm or launcher, never a bow, a crossbow or an admin gun. The pick shows your 2 least and 2 most
  valuable guns (all of them when you carry 4 or fewer), each as "Pistol (T1, quality 3): they'll be grateful" - the
  last part says what it means to them (small thanks, they'll be grateful, a real help to them, they'll remember this,
  they'll never forget it: `EnclaveGunWorthBands`), then *"None of these."*. The gun you pick is taken from its very
  slot, mods and all, and only if it is still there unchanged. Its **tier**: its T0-T5 tag, else a T and a digit in its
  name (IZY's guns), else the NPC weapon catalogue's band + 1, else its value (`EnclaveGunValueBands`). Its **points**:
  3, 6, 10, 15, 18 or 22 for T0-T5 (`EnclaveGunTierPoints`) x (0.75 + 0.1 x quality) (`EnclaveGunQualityBase`,
  `EnclaveGunQualityStep`), rounded, kept within 3-30 (`EnclaveGunPointsMin`/`Max`): a quality-3 T1 pistol +6, a
  quality-5 T3 rifle +19.
- **Done:** the standing, SurvivorOrigins' "favour" Leadership event (60 XP), the ledger line ("The X: a medium bundle
  of medicine delivered (+10 standing)") and their thanks.

**A group that turns hostile or is gone** withdraws its request: no standing change, its journal quest is removed. An
enclave that moves to a new building keeps its request, and the journal marker follows it after the next load.

**Config** (`Config/blocks.xml`, commented there): `EnclaveRequestChanceNeutral`, `EnclaveRequestChanceFriendly`,
`EnclaveRequestChanceAllied`, `EnclaveRequestMaxOpen`, `EnclaveRequestCooldownDays`, `EnclaveOfferLapseDays`,
`EnclaveDueDaysItems`, `EnclaveGeneratorFromDay`, `EnclaveRequestKindWeights`, `EnclaveRequestSizeWeights`,
`EnclaveGainResource`, `EnclaveGainGenerator`, `EnclaveGunTierPoints`, `EnclaveGunQualityBase`, `EnclaveGunQualityStep`,
`EnclaveGunPointsMin`, `EnclaveGunPointsMax`, `EnclaveGunValueBands`, `EnclaveGunWorthBands`, `EnclaveLossDecline`,
`EnclaveLossFail`, `EnclaveLossLapse`. With `Enclaves=false` every request is held as it is.

**Console** (host): `sc enclave requests` (every open one: the enclave, its standing, the kind, what, the state and the
days left), `sc enclave request <id>` (its request, and whether the daily roll can give it one and at what chance),
`sc enclave request <id> offer <resource|generator|gun> [food|meds|ammo|mats|fuel] [small|medium|large] [force]`
(ignores the chance, the cooldown and the generator day; refuses at `EnclaveRequestMaxOpen` without `force`),
`... accept`, `... complete [points]` (the real outcome: standing, journal, Leadership, ledger; a gun's points default
to a quality-3 T1's), `... fail` (an offer is declined, an agreed one fails), `... lapse` (an offer lapses, an agreed
one runs out of time), `... clear` (withdrawn with no standing change, the cooldown cleared too).

**The request clock:** `sc day` simulates the next day each time it is used, but the world's day stays where it is,
so requests keep their own day: it moves one day per `sc day` and otherwise follows the world. While it is ahead of the
world, the world's next days pass for requests only once the world gets there (the log says so once). A world day set
back by the console (`settime`) pulls the request clock, every open request and the journal's due days back with it -
and, since E6, every other enclave day stamp too (see **Allies (E6)**; the log: "enclaves: the world's day went back
from N to M ...").

**Saving:** the requests are `[encrequest]` sections in `survivor_community.txt`, with `encrequestid` and `encreqday`
in `[community]` and `nextreq` (the cooldown) in each `[enclave]`. Forward-only, as E2's: an older build drops them.
A request whose enclave is missing is dropped. The journal quest is the player's own save: on every load SC squares
the two - a request quest whose request is gone is closed, an agreed request whose quest is missing gets it again.

**Limits:** host only, as E2 - a multiplayer client sees no request rows. The request's own words are picked at
runtime by kind and standing; since round vo2 each has its own clip, named after its Localization key, and the
sentence of specifics after it (what, how much, by when) is text only. With `DialogLine=false` the specifics are only
in the journal once you agree.

## Requests, part 2 (E5)

Three more things an enclave can need from you: one of their people found, an infestation cleared, and help when the
dead come over their walls.

**When they ask**
- A **missing survivor** and an **infestation** join the daily roll above (`EnclaveRequestKindWeights`: missing 2,
  infestation 2 next to E4's 6 / 3 / 1), each only where it can be given: someone at home besides the leader who could go
  missing and a building to search; an infestation near them, or room for one. Once you agree you have 3 days, counting
  today (`EnclaveDueDaysSearch`). They count toward `EnclaveRequestMaxOpen` like E4's.
- A **siege** has its own pace (about one a week): each day 15 % (`EnclaveSiegeChance`) that one enclave calls for help
  at a random hour between 8:00 and 20:00 (`EnclaveSiegeHourMin`, `EnclaveSiegeHourMax`) - one you have found, not
  hostile, with no open request, within 2 km of you (`EnclaveSiegeRange`). Only with a **powered radio** at home or at an
  outpost (no radio, no call); never in the blood-moon window (from 10:00 on its day until it ends) nor so close before
  it that the time to answer would still be running when it opens, never while a base attack, or an outpost attack
  where you are, is being fought - and those wait while a siege is fought. One siege at a time; it doesn't count toward
  the open requests.

**A missing survivor**
- The leader says one of theirs went out and never came back. When you agree, they tell you who and where they were
  headed: a building with sleepers 200-800 m away (`EnclaveMissingMin`, `EnclaveMissingMax`) - never an enclave's,
  your home, an outpost, a claimed or infested building, or one another task already sends you to. The journal's
  "Find <name>" points at it.
- The first time you get near that building, it's decided whether they are still alive: 80 % on the day you agreed,
  20 % less each day after, never below 10 % (`EnclaveMissingAlive`, `EnclaveMissingAliveFade`, `EnclaveMissingAliveMin`).
- **Alive:** they hide indoors at one of the building's sleeper spots - often among the dead. Talk to them: *"Your
  people sent me. Let's get you home."* and they follow you (no fee, no time limit, and they don't count toward
  `MaxFollowers`); *"Wait here for me."* makes them stay put. Once they're inside the enclave's grounds they're home:
  +15 standing (`EnclaveGainMissingAlive`), SurvivorOrigins' "rescue" event, and the leader thanks you at the next talk.
  If they die on the way, the request fails (-8 / -8 / -15). If they die before you reach them, their remains lie where
  they fell.
- **Dead:** their remains lie in the building. *"Search the body"* - then the journal says "Tell <enclave> about
  <name>", and the leader's row *"I found <name>. I'm sorry."* ends it: +8 (`EnclaveGainMissingDead`), the "favour" event.
- Run out of time or give up (*"I can't do it."* - not while they follow you or once you found their remains) and
  they're lost for good: the remains go.
- While they may still be alive, their enclave stands even if everyone at home dies (brought home, they lead it); once
  they are lost or found dead with nobody else left, the enclave is gone.

**An infestation to clear**
- The leader names an infestation within 300 m of their grounds (`EnclaveInfestRange`) - one that is already there,
  or one that starts there for the request (the infestations' own rules and cap; a building they refuse stays refused -
  and one that starts within `InfestRadioRange` of home is also reported by a powered radio, a moment before the leader
  names it, as any new one is).
  Hearing of it from the leader puts its purple marker on your map. The journal's "Clear <building>" points at it.
- It's done the moment the infestation is cleared, wherever you are: +15 (`EnclaveGainInfestation`), the "favour"
  event, a tell, and the leader thanks you at the next talk. Cleared before you agreed? *"I already took care of it."*
  ends it on the spot (an offer you never answer is then simply withdrawn, no loss).

**A siege**
- The call: *"The X on the radio: They're coming over the walls! Please, hurry!"*, a ledger line and a tracked journal
  quest, "Siege at The X", counting down live: "14:52 to reach The X". You have 15 real minutes
  (`EnclaveSiegeMinutes`): it is game time at the day's speed, so pausing stops it and sleeping uses it up (with time
  frozen in the sandbox it counts real seconds). Get there while another attack is being fought where you are (a base
  attack next door, an outpost's) and the siege waits for it - its clock stands still until that fight is over.
- Get within 70 m of their grounds (`EnclaveSiegeArriveRange`) and all of their people come out; the horde comes over
  the walls from their far side from you, one a second: (6 + your gamestage x 0.12) x their people / 4 zombies, 4 to 30
  (`EnclaveSiegeBase`, `EnclaveSiegePerGS`, `EnclaveSiegeMin`, `EnclaveSiegeMax`), 25-50 m beyond their grounds
  (`EnclaveSiegeSpawnMin`, `EnclaveSiegeSpawnMax`) within 90 degrees of that side (`EnclaveSiegeSpread`), never closer
  than 30 m to you (`EnclaveSiegeMinPlayerRange`) and never within 50 m in your view (`EnclaveSiegeNoPopRange`). They go
  for the enclave's people; the people fight back, and their deaths are real. The journal counts "8 left".
- **Won:** every one of them dead - +25 (`EnclaveGainSiege`), the "rescue" event, and the leader's thanks. A fight that
  stalls 3 game hours (`EnclaveSiegeMaxHours`) with no kill in the last hour is over: won if you killed any.
- **Lost:** too late, or every one of their people dead - the enclave falls and its building becomes an infestation
  (known, with its marker).
- **Leaving mid-fight** (beyond twice the arrive range): the horde goes, and whether they hold is rolled - the share of
  the horde already dead plus 5 % for each of them still alive (`EnclaveSiegeLeaveRoll`). Held earns nothing (you
  didn't see it through); not held, they fall. Dying and respawning far away counts as leaving.
- Save and reload mid-fight: 15 seconds after the load (`EnclaveSiegeResumeSeconds`) their people still alive are all
  out again and the zombies still standing come back; mid-countdown, the clock carries on where it was.

**Config** (`Config/blocks.xml`, commented there): `EnclaveDueDaysSearch`, `EnclaveMissingMin`, `EnclaveMissingMax`,
`EnclaveMissingAlive`, `EnclaveMissingAliveFade`, `EnclaveMissingAliveMin`, `EnclaveInfestRange`, `EnclaveSiegeChance`,
`EnclaveSiegeHourMin`, `EnclaveSiegeHourMax`, `EnclaveSiegeRange`, `EnclaveSiegeMinutes`, `EnclaveSiegeArriveRange`,
`EnclaveSiegeMaxHours`, `EnclaveSiegeBase`, `EnclaveSiegePerGS`, `EnclaveSiegeMin`, `EnclaveSiegeMax`,
`EnclaveSiegeSpawnMin`, `EnclaveSiegeSpawnMax`, `EnclaveSiegeSpread`, `EnclaveSiegeMinPlayerRange`,
`EnclaveSiegeNoPopRange`, `EnclaveSiegeResumeSeconds`, `EnclaveSiegeLeaveRoll`, `EnclaveGainMissingAlive`,
`EnclaveGainMissingDead`, `EnclaveGainInfestation`, `EnclaveGainSiege`; and `missing`, `infestation` in
`EnclaveRequestKindWeights`.

**Console** (host): `sc enclave request <id> offer missing|infestation` (only where it can be given),
`sc enclave request <id> fate alive|dead` (the missing survivor's fate before you get there, this session),
`sc enclave siege <id>` (the siege clock and why a call would wait), `sc enclave siege <id> now [force]` (a call now:
the pace ignored, the rules kept - `force` skips the radio, the range, discovery and the blood moon),
`... win`, `... lose` (the enclave falls, its building infested), `... clear` (withdrawn: the horde despawns, no
standing change). `sc enclave requests` shows the siege clock.

**Saving:** the request's E5 fields go in its `[encrequest]` (`stage`, `targetname`, `spot`, `spotyaw`, `escort`,
`siege`), the leader's thanks owed in `[enclave] thanks`, a missing person is `state=missing`, and `[community]` keeps
`encsiegeat` (today's scheduled call) and `encremains` (remains still to clear from a chunk that was away). A survivor
following you when you quit waits where they stood when you load again (after a crash: where they last waited - the
building, or where you told them to wait).

**Limits:** host only. The found survivor's escort ignores Voice Control's spoken dismiss (it answers that they stay
with their group); use *"Wait here for me."* or SCore's companion window. When the siege is lost while you are still
near, their people's bodies are taken out of the world (the fall), not left as corpses.

## Allies (E6)

An enclave **Allied** with you (standing 70 or more, not hostile) now does something for your community, and how the
enclaves fare shows in your morale.

**Their benefit** - one per enclave, with State of Decay 2's names, set by the group's kind and kept for good:
- Growers: **Share of the Harvest**, food. Medics: **Open Pharmacy**, medicine. Militia: **Munitions Manufacture**,
  ammo. Builders: **Construction Partners**, building materials. A road crew: **Filtration Service**, fuel. Each is 1
  unit into the stockpile at each day rollover (`EnclaveBenefitBundles`), before the day's needs are taken; the day's
  summary says "allies sent +1 food".
- Scavengers have one of these, rolled once (the same on every load of the save):
  - **Comfort in Numbers:** +5 morale (`EnclaveBenefitMorale`), a morale part of its own.
  - **Overflow Barracks:** +3 beds (`EnclaveBenefitBeds`) wherever beds count against your survivors - the beds part of
    morale and the community window ("Beds 5 for 4 survivors ... (+3 from allies)").
  - **Fight Club / Gun Safety Club / Jogging Buddies / Scavenging Partners:** your survivors earn twice the Fighting /
    Shooting / Cardio / Wits XP (`EnclaveBenefitClubXp`).
  - **Training and Advice:** half as much XP again in every skill and in the specialty (`EnclaveBenefitTrainingXp`).
  - **Neighborhood Watch:** no new infestation within 500 m of them (`EnclaveWatchRange`) - not from the daily roll, a
    spread or an enclave's request - and one already within that range spreads at half the chance (`EnclaveWatchSpread`).
- Raiders have none.
- It works only while they are Allied. Let them down and they are Friendly again (E4's losses): the benefit stops until
  they are Allied once more. Gained or lost, a tell and a ledger line say so ("You and The X are allies now: Share of the
  Harvest (+1 food a day).").
- Several allies add up: two Fight Clubs make Fighting XP x3, a Fight Club and Training and Advice x2.5. The allies' XP
  multiplier is at most x3 (`EnclaveBenefitXpCap`); traits, high morale and Leadership multiply on top as before.
- Their leader mentions it in *"Tell me about this place."*; `sc enclave info <id>` and the radio window show it too.

**Morale** (in the community window's morale parts and `sc morale`)
- **Allies:** +2 for each Allied enclave, at most +6 (`EnclaveAllyMorale`, `EnclaveAllyMoraleCap`).
- **Comfort in Numbers:** +5 for each such ally, on top of that.
- **Enclave lost:** -5 (`EnclaveLostMorale`) when a Friendly or Allied enclave is lost - wiped out, or fallen to a
  siege - fading a point a day. SurvivorOrigins' Leadership shield shrinks it like the other negative parts.

**A new group where one was lost**
- Every enclave lost, whatever the cause and raiders too, is replaced 5 days later (`EnclaveRefillDays`): a new group
  moves into another building with the late seed's rules - nothing loaded around you, nowhere near a claim, buildings
  nobody has visited first, spaced from the others, about a quarter raiders as before - never the building that was
  lost, and never above the map's enclave count (`EnclavesPerKm2`). When no building fits it tries again the next day.
- They are not found yet. With a powered radio you hear "Word on the radio: a new group has settled near X, 1200 m
  north-east." and the ledger notes it.
- Recruiting can never empty an enclave (E3 always leaves 2), so only a fall leaves one to fill.

**Allies tell you about more**
- On becoming Allied, and then every 7 days while Allied (`EnclaveAllyRevealDays`), an ally tells you about the enclave
  nearest to them that you don't know, raiders included: "Word from The X: The Y, holed up at Z, 900 m north-east.", its
  marker on the map and a ledger line. One that drops to Friendly and back within those days tells you no more.

**The radio window's Enclaves column** (right of the station's own sections; the window is now 1384 wide, still narrower
than the community window)
- **Enclaves you know**, nearest first: the name, the standing in their markers' colours (blue Allied, green Friendly,
  grey Neutral, red Hostile), the benefit (blue while it applies) and where. The 10 nearest (15 before E7's Allies
  panel); a line counts the rest.
- **Their requests:** who, what they need, and the day to answer an offer by or to deliver by (red on the day it is due).
  The 3 soonest (4 before E7).
- **Siege calls:** a call with its countdown ("12:34 left to reach them") and, until the fight starts, two buttons:
  **On our way** (they thank you over the radio; nothing else changes) and **We can't come** (press it twice: the siege
  is lost now - the enclave falls and its building is infested, as when the clock runs out). A fight already on shows
  how many of the dead are left.

**A world day set back** (`settime`, an older save): every enclave day stamp keeps the days it had left instead of
waiting for the world to catch up - the request clock and its requests (E4), refills and ally reveals (E6), the radio's
next reveal (E1), recruit cooldowns and enlistments (E3), the day's siege call (E5) and the infestations' ages (I1). The
log says so in one line ("enclaves: the world's day went back from N to M ..."). Refills and ally reveals count days on
the request clock, so `sc day` moves them as it moves requests.

**Config** (`Config/blocks.xml`, commented there): `EnclaveBenefitBundles`, `EnclaveBenefitMorale`, `EnclaveBenefitBeds`,
`EnclaveBenefitClubXp`, `EnclaveBenefitTrainingXp`, `EnclaveBenefitXpCap`, `EnclaveWatchRange`, `EnclaveWatchSpread`,
`EnclaveAllyMorale`, `EnclaveAllyMoraleCap`, `EnclaveLostMorale`, `EnclaveRefillDays`, `EnclaveAllyRevealDays`.

**Console** (host): `sc enclave info <id>` (the benefit, whether it applies, when they next tell of another),
`sc enclave list` (a line on the allies, what they bring and the refills owed), `sc enclave refill` (the refills owed
and their days), `sc enclave refill now` (every refill owed, now; one anyway with none owed - still never above the
count), `sc morale` (the Allies, Comfort in Numbers and Enclave lost parts). `sc enclave standing <id> 75` makes an ally.

**Saving:** each `[enclave]` gains `benefit` and `allyreveal`; `[community]` gains `encloss` (the Enclave lost part)
and `encrefill` (the refills owed). An older build ignores the keys, and its next save drops them.

**Limits:** host only, as the rest of the enclaves. The allies' bundles come with SC's day rollover, which a dedicated
server does not run. "On our way" is remembered for the session only.

## Gifts and ally barter (E7)

**Gifts.** An enclave leader's greeting has *"I brought you something."* It lists up to four bundles you carry, the
smallest of each resource ("A medium bundle of medicine"; the ones they don't live on first, then their own, then
luxuries), and the one you pick is theirs: +3 standing whatever its size (`EnclaveGiftStanding`), the leader's thanks by
standing with "(standing +3)", and a ledger line ("gift to The X: a medium bundle of medicine (+3 standing)"). One gift
per enclave a day (`EnclaveGiftDays`, on the requests' clock, so `sc day` moves it). A gift never lifts standing past 69
(`EnclaveGiftCap`; 100 for no cap): gifts can win a group to Friendly or mend a falling-out, but an alliance still takes
their requests. The row greys with the reason: "Come back tomorrow.", "You aren't carrying any bundles." or, at the cap,
"They'd rather you helped with what they need." Any SC bundle counts, an open request's resource too (its own
hand-over row stays separate). `EnclaveGifts=false` hides the row.

**Ally barter.** The radio window has an **Allies** panel between the requests and the siege calls. Its first button
picks one of your Allied enclaves, nearest first (press it for the next), with what they do for you and where. Under it,
**Give** cycles through what you send from the stockpile (food, medical, ammo, materials, fuel or water), **Get** shows
what they make (growers food, medics medical, militia ammo, builders materials, a road crew fuel; a scavengers' group
lets you pick any of those five), and **Trade** swaps 2 of yours for 1 of theirs (`EnclaveBarterGive`,
`EnclaveBarterGet`): "Traded 2 ammo for 1 food with The X." and a ledger line. Once a day per ally
(`EnclaveBarterDays`, the requests' clock); only Allied groups trade ("Only allies trade this way." when one drops to
Friendly while the window is open). The radio's own barter (3 for 1 with anyone, once a day) stays as it was. The radio
window still opens only at a powered radio block. To make room the Enclaves list shows the 10 nearest and the requests
3; E9's radio commands will add their buttons to the same panel.

**Config** (`Config/blocks.xml`, commented there): `EnclaveGifts`, `EnclaveGiftStanding`, `EnclaveGiftDays`,
`EnclaveGiftCap`, `EnclaveBarter`, `EnclaveBarterGive`, `EnclaveBarterGet`, `EnclaveBarterDays`.

**Console** (host): `sc enclave gift <id>` (the gift state, the leader's row now and the rows you would see),
`sc enclave gift <id> give <res> [small|medium|large] [force]` (a gift as if handed over, no bundle needed; `force`
skips the day's wait, never the cap), `sc enclave barter <id>` (what they trade and when), `sc enclave barter <id>
<give> [get]` (one trade now, the day's wait ignored as `sc barter` does). `sc enclave info <id>` has a gifts and
barter line.

**Saving:** each `[enclave]` gains `gift` and `barter` (the requests' day the next one is taken), written when set. An
older build ignores them, and its next save drops them. A world day set back moves them back with the other request-
clock stamps.

**Limits:** host only, as the rest of the enclaves; single player is what SC supports.

## Infestations (I1)

Some buildings get overrun. An **infested** building holds about twice the zombies, more of them feral, and something
worse in one of its rooms, until you clear it. Infestations start on their own, spread when left alone, and go on the
map once you know about them.

**When and where**
- **At each day rollover** there is a 40% chance (`InfestDailyChance`) that one more building becomes infested.
- **Which buildings:** real POIs with sleepers, at least `InfestTraderDistance` (150 m) from a trader. Never one
  someone has claimed (a land claim, a bedroll, an upgraded claim zone, an outpost), an enclave's building, your home
  zone, a building a trader quest has locked, one SC or you are using (a radio contact, a recruit's "secure this
  building" favour, a supply run's destination, a radio scouting mark, one of your quests in progress), one with a
  player within `InfestPlayerMargin` (50 m), or one with a room still live: nobody watches a building turn. It needs a
  room that coming near sets off (a building of trap rooms alone is never picked). Nor one within 500 m of an ally
  keeping a Neighborhood Watch (E6), where a spread is also half as likely.
- **The tier rises with the days** (`InfestTierByDay`): tier 1-2 in the first week, 1-3 in the second, 2-4 from day 15,
  3-5 from day 29.
- **Close enough to matter:** a building 300-2,500 m from home (from you, before you have a home; `InfestMinDist`,
  `InfestMaxDist`) is ten times likelier than one outside that band (`InfestOutsideWeight` 0.1).
- **Spread:** an infestation 3 days old or more (`InfestSpreadDays`) has a 30% chance a day (`InfestSpreadChance`) to
  spread to the nearest such building within 300 m (`InfestSpreadRange`), of any tier. At most once a day.
- **The cap:** at most about 0.2 per km² at once (`InfestPerKm2`; at least `InfestMin` 2, at most `InfestMax` 30): 8 on
  a 6k map, 13 on 8k. At the cap, nothing new starts or spreads.

**What changes inside** (when a room spawns, so it works in buildings you never visited, and after a reload)
- **About twice the zombies** (`InfestMultiplier`; 1.5 times in tier 4-5 buildings, `InfestMultiplierHighTier` from
  `InfestHighTier`), as vanilla's infested trader quests do. A trader quest's own larger number is never lowered.
- **Vanilla's infested spawn points open** (346 vanilla POIs have them).
- **Ferals:** 35% of the normal zombies come as their feral version (`InfestFeralShare`), at any gamestage. Vanilla's
  POI lists have no ferals at all below gamestage 76.
- **One special** in one of its rooms (`InfestSpecial`): a radiated zombie below gamestage 50
  (`InfestSpecialRadiatedBelowGS`, the room's gamestage), then a charged or infernal one. None when the sandbox's
  maximum enemy type is below radiated; only radiated ones when it stops below charged. If its room ends without it (a
  crawler's spawn point, too many zombies about), the next room owes it.
- **A building you'd already cleared is dangerous again:** its rooms respawn the next time you come near them. If you
  were within 100 m when it turned (`InfestRearmPlayerRange`), its rooms wait first: a room fills up again only after
  about 2.5 minutes of game time away from it (1000 world-time, vanilla's own wait for a room someone is in) and then
  when you come within 8 m of it, at spawn points out of your sight. A trap room (one a door or a switch sets off)
  comes back only if you set it off again.
- Ferals and the special that you leave alive are still there when you come back, as any sleeper is.
- The loot stays the building's own. Vanilla's infested crate needs the building rebuilt, which SC never does.

**Clearing it:** clear every sleeper room of the building (you, your followers or your turrets) and the infestation
ends: "You've cleared the infestation at X.", a ledger line, and its marker goes. A room the game closed without
spawning anything because too many zombies were about (vanilla's enemy cap) doesn't count: it respawns when you come
back. A room whose own roll came out empty does count, as vanilla's clear quests count it. A trap room is the exception both ways: once cleared (or closed empty) it counts as cleared unless its door or
switch sets it off again.

**The zombie-cap fix (I2, every building).** The game spawns a sleeper room only while fewer than `MaxSpawnedZombies` x
2.1 enemies are about, counted over the whole world (about 134 by default). Vanilla closes a room you enter while it is
full as cleared with nobody spawned and never respawns it, and a room the cap cuts short closes early. Now such an empty
room is due again about a game hour later (`SleeperCapFix`, `SleeperCapRetryTicks` 1000) and spawns the next time you
come near it after that; one an older build saved that way is repaired the first time you come near it. In an
infested building a room the cap cuts short waits while you are near it, at most about 3 game hours
(`SleeperCapPause`, `SleeperCapPauseMaxTicks` 3000), and goes on once zombies die off, so the special still comes;
with nobody near, or after the wait, it closes as vanilla's. Enclave buildings are never touched. Nothing is saved, and
it works on a dedicated server (it runs where the rooms spawn). `sc sleepers` shows what it did, `sc sleepers scan`
lists the rooms still waiting for a repair, and `sc sleepers repair` makes them due now.

**Seeing them** (an infestation shows on the map only once you know about it)
- Come within `InfestDiscoverRange` (150 m) of it: "Infested: X. More of them, ferals, and something worse."
- A **powered radio** reports a new one within `InfestRadioRange` (2 km) of home (of you, without a home), and where:
  "The radio picks up a warning: X is infested, 900 m north-east."
- A spread from one you know about is known too: "The infestation at X has spread to Y."
- **Marker** (owner-only): a purple infection symbol labelled "Infested: X", on the map, and on the compass within 300 m.

**Near home (I3).** An infestation within 500 m of your home's land claim block (`InfestHomeRange`, measured flat to the
building's nearest edge) is a nest near home. Your people spot one the moment it starts or spreads there
(`InfestHomeKnown`): its marker, "Your people spotted the dead moving in close to home. X is infested, 420 m north." and
a ledger line; on loading a save, and when you move your home, any one already that close that you didn't know of is
spotted the same way. Each nest costs morale by its distance (`InfestHomeMoraleBands`: 5 within 150 m, 3 to 300 m, 2 to
500 m), the nearest 5 counted (`InfestHomeMoraleNests`), at most 15 in all (`InfestHomeMoraleCap`): the morale part
"Infestations near home", which SurvivorOrigins' Leadership shields like the other losses. Each also draws the dead: +1
attention a game hour (`InfestHomeAttention`), at most 5 nests counted (`InfestHomeAttentionCap`), so a quiet base
(-2 an hour) holds steady with two nests and fills with three or more; manned watch posts cut it, and the blood moon
and an open attack pause it, as they do the noise's. Clear a nest and both go at once. Nothing is saved: the nests are
counted from the infestations. `sc morale` names each nest with its distance and cost, `sc noise` and `sc infest list`
show them too.

**Config** (`Config/blocks.xml`, commented there): the switch `Infestations`; how often and how many
`InfestDailyChance`, `InfestPerKm2`, `InfestMin`, `InfestMax`; spread `InfestSpreadDays`, `InfestSpreadChance`,
`InfestSpreadRange`; which buildings `InfestTraderDistance`, `InfestMinDist`, `InfestMaxDist`, `InfestOutsideWeight`,
`InfestTierByDay`, `InfestPlayerMargin`; inside `InfestMultiplier`, `InfestMultiplierHighTier`, `InfestHighTier` (the
multipliers go from 1 to 4), `InfestFeralShare`, `InfestSpecial`, `InfestSpecialRadiatedBelowGS`,
`InfestRearmPlayerRange`; seeing them `InfestDiscoverRange`, `InfestRadioRange`; near home `InfestHomeRange`,
`InfestHomeMoraleBands`, `InfestHomeMoraleNests`, `InfestHomeMoraleCap`, `InfestHomeAttention`,
`InfestHomeAttentionCap`, `InfestHomeKnown`.

**Console** (host only): `sc infest [list]` (each one's building, tier, start day and age, whether you know of it, its
special and where it is from you), `sc infest info <id>` (the record and the building's rooms: generated, cleared,
alive, due, the special's room), `sc infest add [<x> <z>]` (infests the building you stand in, or the one at x z, at
once: no roll, no tier or distance rules, but never an enclave's building, your home, a claimed building or one
already infested; known, with its marker), `sc infest clear <id|all>` (ends it: the record and the marker only, the
rooms are left as they are), `sc infest spread <id>` (spreads now; the cap holds), `sc infest roll` (the daily roll
now, without the chance; the cap and every rule hold), `sc infest reveal <id|all>` / `hide <id|all>`. `sc poi` adds a
line when the building you stand in is infested.

**Saving:** the infestations are `[infestation]` sections in `survivor_community.txt`, plus `infestid` in
`[community]`. This is **forward-only**: an older SurvivorCommunity build skips the `[infestation]` sections as
unreadable (a "degraded" load), ignores the key, and its next save drops them all.

**Multiplayer:** the host rolls, spreads and clears them, and the changes inside apply to every player's rooms (the
server spawns the sleepers). Finding them, the markers and the radio are the host's. A dedicated server runs no SC day
rollover (it has no local player), so nothing new starts, spreads or is found there; infestations already in the save
still change their rooms and still end when cleared.

**Switching them off and uninstalling:** with `Infestations=false` the records stay, but nothing starts or spreads, the
rooms spawn as vanilla's and no marker shows. Uninstalling is safe: nothing SC changes in a room needs SC to load. The
special is saved under vanilla's group names, ferals are vanilla classes, and a room made due again just respawns as
vanilla's.

## Survivors with the player's body

- Player-body NPCs (`Config/entityclasses.xml`) wear the **player character model**: a random face, race,
  hair, eye colour and (for men) facial hair from the game's own character catalogue, plus **real vanilla
  armor** in their equipment slots - the same meshes you wear. It protects them at half the rating it would
  give you, and the framework's flat difficulty resistance tags (`DRLow/Mid/High`) are left off because the
  armor replaces them: stacked, a raider in three medium pieces measured 84.5% and barely noticed a bat. An
  unarmored one now sits at 5%, a full top set under 50% (`WORN_ARMOR_SCALE` in `Tools/gen_arsenal.py`). The
  armor does not break, and wear is not kept across loads.
- **Survivors are 0-XNPCCore's own.** Since the XNPCCore migration the community's survivors are 0-XNPCCore 3.2's
  player-body survivors, `npcSdcsSurvivor{Male,Female}T{1,2,3}` (`XnpcSurvivorClasses`), dressed, named, healed and
  armed by this mod; SurvivorCommunity no longer defines survivor classes of its own. **A save from before the
  migration needs a new game:** its members were `scSurvivor` classes, which are gone, so they can never be found or
  re-created; the log says `this save predates the XNPCCore migration: start a new game` when such a save loads,
  and again when the daily tick finally removes them.
- **Following is 0-XNPCCore's; voice orders are member states.** A following member uses 0-XNPCCore's own follow
  (`DynamicFollow`): its formations, follow distances and engage stances. The community's own follow actions are only
  a fallback for a pack NPC that lacks it. The order wheel and `@name` commands work on followers - **stay**, **guard**,
  **guard and return**, **wander**, **show me a route** / **patrol**, crouch, formations, stances - and the member
  simply holds that order, still following you (the window shows *Following (holding)*, *(guarding)* ...) until you
  call them (`@name follow`) or fetch them; none of it ever counts as leaving, and a spoken *dismiss* sends them home.
  A member holding a spot outside the base is not stationed when you walk home without them, and is never counted
  missing. Stationing clears 0-XNPCCore's formation, move-to and crouch state; the stance and follow distance stay.
- **Hired companions may join.** A companion you hired the vanilla way can be asked to join like anyone (do the
  favour first); joining ends the paid hire - members are never billed. Somebody else's hire cannot be recruited.
- **No 0-XNPCCore kill levels for members.** Members and their stand-ins grow through the community's skills only;
  0-XNPCCore's level bonus (health, damage, spread, regeneration) and NPC progression are kept off them. Strangers
  keep 0-XNPCCore's levels, and a member who leaves gets them back.
- **Runners on 0-XNPCCore bodies** run at `RunnerRunMps` (4.5 m/s) and walk at `RunnerWalkMps` (1.6 m/s) in the
  world, in literal metres per second, with Cardio, hunger and injury applied on top.
- **A member's inventory window** (their ammo and medical stock) drops as a backpack where they die. If you have it
  open then, it closes; if another player has it open, its contents drop at the body in a second backpack once they
  close it (or leave the game), so nothing in it can be taken twice.
- **Raiders (M10): the survivors' body, hostile.** Raiders are six classes on 0-XNPCCore's player body, the same
  body as the survivors: `scRaider{Male,Female}T{1,2,3}`, the tier being the band they turned up in (T1 early, T2
  middle, T3 late). They are hostile by class (the `bandits` faction, which hates you, your survivors and the
  zombies, never another raider), cannot be talked to or recruited (looking at one shows no talk prompt, and E does
  nothing), and use 0-XNPCCore's fighting AI with real
  vanilla weapons, plus a small package of this mod's (`SCRaider`: closing in until the gun or bow reaches, and
  going to look when they hear you). Voice Control leaves them alone (`no_voice`). They get this mod's faces and
  rougher clothes (`RaiderGearSets`), names like "Rook the Raider", items-only healing, the aim pose, and no
  0-XNPCCore kill levels (`RaiderXnpcLevels` false). They open unlocked doors (SCore's opener, which shuts them
  again; `RaiderVanillaDoors` adds the vanilla one); locked doors stop them. Your turrets shoot them as enemies, as
  they shot the old raiders: the powered auto turret with "target zombies" on, the junk turret and junk sledge with
  "target enemies" on, and a motion sensor set to "target zombies" trips on them (`RaiderTurretsAsEnemies`; false
  leaves only "target strangers", and survivors, members and traders are never affected). A raider spawned as a sleeper shows the red dot of a clear quest, like a zombie.
- The raider classes, the spawn groups, the `XnpcWeapons` list and their names are **generated** by
  `Tools/gen_arsenal.py` from one table; edit the table and rerun it rather than the XML. `Tools/verify_arsenal.py`
  replays the framework's and this mod's patches and checks every class, AI package, archetype, spawn-group entry,
  name and duplicate.
- **Gear follows the class tier, the gamestage picks the class** (2026-10-09: "T2 NPCs must not get T0 gear"). Every
  weapon follows the tier in its item name, which Combat Sandbox also shows ("Wooden Club (T0)", "AK-47 Machine Gun
  (T1)"): T1 NPCs carry T0 and T1 weapons (T1 a little rarer: 61/39%), T2 NPCs T1 and T2 (61/39%), T3 NPCs T2, T3 and
  T4 (42/42/17% with IZY's T4 guns, else 50/50) - melee weapons, bows and guns alike. A row's `bands=` gives its tier
  group's weight for T1/T2/T3 NPCs and `group=` names the group; a group's weapons split that weight, so a tier with
  many weapons is no likelier than one with few. Clothes use the same tier (quality 1-2 / 2-4 / 3-5; the Primitive
  starter armor only on T1 NPCs, `GearSetLastTier`). The gamestage no longer lowers an NPC's gear: it picks the class
  as it spawns instead - a spawn group's T2 or T3 survivor or raider above your gamestage's tier (T1 below 50, T2 below
  100) comes in as the T1 or T2 one of the same sex (`XnpcClassByGamestage`, a postfix on the game's entity-group
  pick), and the radio and outpost raids already pick their class by gamestage. `sc spawn T3` makes a T3 at any
  gamestage.
- **IZY Classic's guns** (with `IGP_Classic` and Combat Sandbox installed): 51 more guns and a crossbow for survivors
  and raiders alike, appended to `XnpcWeapons` by a conditional block (also generated by `gen_arsenal.py`), by the
  same tier rule as every weapon. With them a T3 NPC's bows are 29% IZY's VH automatic crossbow (T4, its own
  group). Left out: IZY's melee weapons, the
  dual pistols (no aim or reload pose on a body), the grenade launchers, the minigun, the flamethrower, the nailgun
  and what Combat Sandbox marks not ready. Locked & Loaded's IZY Classic magazines arm the 38 with detachable
  magazines; revolvers, bolt-actions, tube shotguns, the musket and the crossbow start with loose rounds, as vanilla's
  do. IZY's replacement Hunting Rifle lost vanilla's `ranged` tag, so NPCs took it for a club: `Config/items.xml` puts
  `T1,weapon,ranged` back while IZY replaces it (your own rifle's ranged perks and mods count it again too). A raider's
  kill XP follows the new tiers too: one carrying vanilla's AK-47, Pistol, Hunting Rifle or Double Barrel (now T1) is
  worth +100% instead of +133%. Without the pack none of this exists. A separate military faction carrying military weapons only is planned, not built.
- **Two weapons each, or a melee weapon alone.** Every survivor and every raider carries one melee and one ranged
  weapon (a raider's `RaiderRangedShare` only decides which of the two it leads with), except the 15% who carry only
  the melee weapon (`SurvivorMeleeOnlyChance`, `RaiderMeleeOnlyChance`; such a raider always leads with it). Hand a
  melee-only survivor a gun or bow and it becomes their ranged weapon. Ranged weapons are mostly bows and crossbows: a
  survivor's is a firearm only `SurvivorFirearmChance` (12%) of the time, a raider's `RaiderFirearmChance` (12%).
  - A **survivor** carries real vanilla weapons, both rolled for that NPC from the generated `XnpcWeapons` list
    (the raiders' weapons and tiers as their vanilla items, without fists and the rocket launcher: 41, in
    `Config/blocks.xml`), by its class's tier (T1 early, T2 middle, T3 late - the place and time of day it turned up,
    brought down to your gamestage's as it spawns). It fights
    with 0-XNPCCore's own weapon handling: the gun while it has ammo, the melee weapon up close and when the gun
    runs dry. The pair is saved with the NPC and, for a prospect or member, in the registry (`loadout=`), so every
    body the community re-creates for them (a call-back, a stand-in, a runner) carries the same two.
  - **Ammo is finite.** A new survivor starts with `XnpcSpawnAmmo` (30) rounds of its gun's basic ammo, or
    `XnpcSpawnArrows` (20) arrows or bolts, in the inventory window you see when you open them, and what it shoots
    is gone. Hand them more through that window whenever you like. (Ammo put in the window moves to their bag, where a
    bow can shoot it from, within a few seconds - but never while anyone has the window open, in multiplayer too, so
    nothing in it can be taken twice. On a dedicated server it moves when the window is closed. While the window is
    open they also don't bandage or swap weapons from it.)
  - **The stockpile refills members - only at home or at an outpost.** A member (or a posted member's stand-in)
    whose gun is down to fewer than `XnpcResupplyBelow` (10) rounds while standing inside the base's claim or at one
    of your outposts gets `XnpcResupplyAmmo` (30) rounds, or `XnpcResupplyArrows` (20) arrows or bolts, from the
    stockpile's ammo, at most once a `XnpcResupplyCooldown` (60 s). Out in the world nobody refills them: once the
    gun is dry they fight on with their melee weapon until you bring them home or hand them ammo. Strangers are never
    refilled. The stockpile pays in packing points: a round costs what it is worth at the packing station (its
    economic value, times `XnpcResupplyPointsMult`, 1), and an ammo bundle holds `PointsPerBundleAmmo` (2000) points,
    opened only when the last one is used up - about 220 9 mm rounds or 165 stone arrows a bundle. The log names
    every refill; `sc xnpc` counts them. With Locked & Loaded a member who carries magazines counts the rounds in
    them too, and the refill goes into those magazines: the stockpile pays only for the rounds they take, and nothing
    when they are full.
  - **Nobody comes back with new ammo.** A body the community re-creates for a member (a call-back, an outpost
    stand-in, a rescue stand-in, a runner's fresh body) starts with the rounds that member last carried (kept in the
    registry as `ammo=`), not a new survivor's 30 - and with Locked & Loaded, with the same magazines holding the
    same rounds (the magazines ride in the same `ammo=` value; an older SurvivorCommunity reading it keeps the loose
    rounds and skips them). If Locked & Loaded is gone by then, or one of the magazine kinds is, its rounds come back
    loose instead.
  - **Magazines, with Locked & Loaded** (1.4 or later). An NPC that carries a magazine for its gun reloads like you:
    the fullest magazine goes in and the old one comes back with what it had left. So with Locked & Loaded installed
    a new survivor's or raider's gun that takes magazines starts with Locked & Loaded's loaded magazines for its
    tier (T1 2, T2 3, T3 4; at T2 and T3 some are extended magazines or drums) instead of the loose rounds above.
    Of the weapons they carry, Locked & Loaded 1.4 has magazines for the pistol, the desert vulture, the SMG, the pipe
    machine gun, the AK-47, the tactical rifle, the M60, the sniper rifle and the auto shotgun. Bows, crossbows and
    the guns without magazines (the pipe pistol, pipe rifle and pipe shotgun, the double barrel, the hunting rifle,
    the lever-action rifle, the pump shotgun, the magnum) start with `XnpcSpawnAmmo` / `RaiderSpawnAmmo` as before,
    and so does every gun without Locked & Loaded. Loose rounds of its gun's ammo that a member has - handed over
    through their window, or from a save made before - go into their magazines whenever those have room, so none is
    dead weight; magazines for their gun handed over through the window go into their bag, where they use them. A
    member whose gun takes magazines but who has none (one from an older save, or given such a gun through the
    weapon menu) is issued empty ones the first time they are at home or at an outpost with it, and the stockpile
    fills them. `remags npc <id>` (Locked & Loaded's console) shows an NPC's magazines; `sc raiders` and
    `sc xnpc <id>` count them.
  - **A gun put away keeps its rounds.** 0-XNPCCore swaps the gun for the melee weapon up close, and the rounds left
    in the gun used to vanish with it (up to a magazine's worth per melee bout). Now they go back to the NPC's bag as
    loose rounds and are loaded again when the gun comes back (with Locked & Loaded the magazine itself goes back,
    as Locked & Loaded does it). The same for the gun an NPC is holding when it dies: its rounds drop in the
    backpack. And the gun in its hand is saved with it: the game keeps only the name of what an NPC holds, so every
    time its chunk unloaded behind you (or the game was saved and loaded) it came back with an empty gun, and with
    Locked & Loaded a magazine fewer. Now it comes back holding the same gun, loaded the same, magazine and all.
  - **Giving a member a better weapon.** Put the weapon in their inventory window and pick it in the dialog's weapon
    menu: 0-XNPCCore's own "Use ..." lines cover vanilla weapons only, so SC adds "Use the weapons I gave you." (shown
    while the window holds one they can carry - IZY Classic's guns included; it takes the highest-tier gun or bow and
    melee weapon there, one of each, the ranged one into the hand), or tell them to use it by voice: they keep it for good in place of their weapon of that kind
    (the registry's `loadout=` follows), and the weapon it replaces comes back to you as a real item - into the
    base's storage boxes when they are loaded and have room, else your inventory, else a bag at your feet; any mods
    on the weapon you gave come back too. Only the weapons the survivors can carry (`XnpcWeapons`) are kept this way;
    anything else they just hold for now. The handed weapon's own quality is not kept (the NPC's copy is rolled 1-6,
    as 0-XNPCCore's are), and a gun given this way brings no ammo of its own.
  - **Overwhelmed, they keep shooting.** 0-XNPCCore puts the gun away for the melee weapon when an enemy is within
    3 m. With enough enemies on them within `OverwhelmedRadius` (12 m) a survivor keeps the gun instead, or goes back
    to it, and backs off as it shoots, as long as it has rounds: 2 enemies for a 1-2 star fighter, 3 at 3-4 stars, 4
    at 5-6, 5 at 7 (`OverwhelmedMin` / `OverwhelmedMax` / `OverwhelmedStarsPerStep`), 3 for anyone who is not a
    member (`OverwhelmedStrangers`). It lasts `OverwhelmedHoldSeconds` (5) after the count drops. From the melee
    weapon they go back to the gun only once that many have been on them for `OverwhelmedSustainSeconds` (1.5 s) -
    one more with the zombie within 1.5 m - and then keep the gun at least `OverwhelmedGunSeconds` (6 s), so a
    zombie turning between you and them no longer flips their weapon back and forth (each return to the gun costs a
    reload). `Overwhelmed` false leaves 0-XNPCCore's swap alone.
  - **Melee as fast as the weapon allows.** Survivors and raiders swing their melee weapon at its own rate - the bat
    about every 1.25 s, as yours - instead of every 2 s on the NPC framework's attack ticks, and from the weapon's
    real reach less `MeleeReachMargin` (the bat: about 2 m, not the framework's 1.5 m), so they no longer step into a
    zombie's reach first or go back to walking every time a hit knocks it back. They keep the melee weapon while
    any enemy is within 3 m and for 1.5 s after their target falls, instead of drawing the gun between two zombies.
    `MeleePacingRaiders` false keeps raiders on the old pace, `MeleePacing` false everyone; `MeleeReach` false gives
    back the framework's 1.5 m. Guns keep their pace.
  - **Shooting skill aims the gun.** Survivors and raiders no longer hit everything they see. Each one hits nearly every
    shot within 5 m per Shooting star (5 m at 1 star, 35 m at 7) and fewer and fewer beyond it, and opens fire only out
    to twice that (at least 12 m, never past the gun's range) - though one being shot at fires back at its attacker
    out to the gun's range, mostly missing. Shots go at the chest. Strangers' stars come from the
    profile they get when they spawn, raiders' are rolled by tier (1-2, 2-4, 4-6). `ShootingSkill` false gives back the

    `ShootingAimChest`, `ShootingDefaultStars` and `RaiderShootingStars` tune it.
  - **The gun stays up.** The aim pose now also holds while a resident waits for you to step out of its line of
    fire and while it backs away from a zombie its gun reaches (it lowers the gun only while it faces away), and its
    arms keep their carry pose: a body whose animator lost it (arms hanging, the gun at the hip even while shooting)
    gets it back at once (`sc aim <name>` counts the repairs, `carryFixes`).
  - **Not yet: holstering.** Survivors keep their weapon in hand when calm. 0-XNPCCore's controller drives their
    weapon pose and swaps the hand item several times a fight, so hiding the weapon alone would leave them miming
    an invisible gun; a proper holster (and the sling the plan wants) is a later task.
  - A **raider** rolls both weapons from the same list at the same tier cap, and a lead: `RaiderRangedShare` of them
    (30% / 40% / 50% for T1 / T2 / T3) rely on the ranged weapon and fight like a survivor (melee within 3 m, back to
    the gun at 5 m); the rest lead with the melee weapon - they charge in, and take the gun or bow only when you stay
    out of reach (farther than `RaiderLeadRangedDist`, 8 m, above them or where their path cannot get, for
    `RaiderLeadRangedSeconds`, 3 s), back to melee once you are within 3 m. Every raider starts with
    `RaiderSpawnAmmo` (24) rounds or `RaiderSpawnArrows` (15) arrows or bolts in its bag (with Locked & Loaded, a gun
    that takes magazines starts with its tier's loaded magazines instead) and is **never resupplied**:
    once the gun is dry it fights with its melee weapon. When it dies it drops a
    backpack with its leftover ammo and medical items, and each of its two weapons is in it `RaiderWeaponDropChance`
    (25%) of the time. The rocket launcher is never rolled. Killing one pays 600 XP, more the better its ranged
    weapon: a bow or crossbow 798 (tier 0) or 1002, a firearm 900, 1200 or 1398 (tier 0, 1, 2) - the old raiders'
    range (before party and difficulty scaling; `sc raiders` shows each one's `killXp`).
  - `sc loadout <name>` shows the pair and `sc loadout <name> <weapon> [weapon]` changes it (vanilla item names or
    their short names; `ranged-lead` / `melee-lead` for a raider; `sc weapons` lists them), `sc loadout near [r]` lists the pairs
    around you, `sc arms <name> melee|ranged` switches by hand. How: the NPC's own starting-item list gets both
    weapons before SCore copies it into the inventory, which is what lets SCore's weapon swap and its save of the
    held weapon accept either one.
- You meet them three ways. They spawn in the world on their **own layer** (below), every radio call brings
  one (the radio names the ranged weapon they carry), and `sc spawn [hostile] [male|female] [T1|T2|T3] [weapon]
  [ranged-lead|melee-lead] [Set]` or the creative menu puts one in front of you (`sc weapons` lists the weapon names;
  `melee`, `bow` or `firearm` picks one of that family). `sc raiders [r]` lists the raiders around you.
- **No new pack NPCs.** While this mod is installed, the NPC framework's sample cast (Nurse, Baker, Harley, the
  NPC fox) and any character pack's NPCs filed in the same groups no longer spawn; ours are the only NPCs that
  turn up. Those already in a world stay and behave as before, and their classes still load. The generator
  removes the framework's own biome spawn entries, and in every framework group (the POI sleeper groups behind
  `S_-Group_NPC_*` included) it swaps each pack NPC for our class of its weapon's tier and its side, since a group
  may not be empty: in a friendly group the 0-XNPCCore survivor class, in a hostile one our raider class. 0-XNPCCore's own survivors stay where its groups list them, but its own biome spawns of them are removed:
  they turn up at this mod's rates. Spawning one by hand (creative menu, test spawn cubes) still works. The radio
  and outpost walk-ins roll only our survivors, so `SurvivorLookChance` is no longer used.
- **Wild spawns are ours, not a slice of the framework's.** They used to be appended into the NPC framework's
  own groups, where they shared the 3 points of NPC against 97 points of nothing that those groups carry and
  came out at 0.4% - you could roam for an hour and meet nobody. They now have their own entity groups and
  their own `<spawn>` entries in all five biomes (`Config/spawning.xml`), which is what the framework's own
  guide asks add-ons to do, so the rate is ours alone and does not move when character packs are added or
  removed. The world is diced into 80x80 m areas; an area rolls one spawn entry at a time against its group,
  and a miss burns that entry for the area until `respawndelay` has passed. So the knobs are exactly: the odds
  of one roll producing somebody (`SURVIVOR_CHANCE` 6%, `RAIDER_CHANCE` 4%; the framework's own cast, now
  removed, rolled about 1-4%)
  and how often an area may roll again (`SURVIVOR_RESPAWN` half a game day, `RAIDER_RESPAWN` one). Which band
  an area draws from follows the framework's layout: wilderness has the early band by day and the middle one
  by night, towns the early band by day and the late one at night, city centres the last two. A survivor band is
  its tier's two classes (T1, T2, T3), whose weapons are rolled as above, and so is a raider band (its raiders lead
  with the melee weapon 70% of the time in the early band, 60% in the middle, 50% late).
  `core SurvivorCommunityDev spawns` prints the live odds, the entries for the biome you are standing in and that
  area's counters.
- The look and the four armor pieces are stored in the registry for prospects and members and re-applied on
  every load (vanilla never saves or sends a non-player's equipment). A survivor sent on a run that radios
  for help appears in the rescue event wearing their own face. `sc look <name>` rerolls a member's face,
  `sc gear <name> <Set|Outfit|none>` dresses them in a whole set or mod outfit, and `sc gear sim [n] [hostile]
  [T1|T2|T3] [any] [male|female]` rolls clothes without making anyone.
- **Paints and tattoos** (with Vanity Vault installed): a new survivor rarely comes with a body paint set (1%; a
  body paint has light skin baked in, so it brings its matching face paint) or a face paint alone (2%), and one
  in seven has a tattoo (dark inks); raiders far more often (6%, 10%, 35%) and from wilder lists (skeleton,
  Harley, clown, Joker, war paint, oni and KISS masks). The picks are yours (`SurvivorPaints_Choices.md`, 2026-10-07),
  kept in the look with the rest of the face and put on after every body build through Vanity Vault's own code
  (`ScVanity.cs`, by reflection: without Vanity Vault there are no paints and nothing else changes). Survivors
  already met keep their looks. `sc paint <name>` shows a survivor's paints and the lists; `sc paint <name> body
  <id>` / `face <id>` / `tattoo <shape>[/ink]` / `none` / `roll` sets them and rebuilds the body. Each paint
  costs its textures once (a few to ~50 MB, shared by everyone wearing it), loaded the first time it shows; with
  every picked paint and tattoo turning up in one session that is about 350-400 MB. A survivor rebuilt while out
  of view gets their paints when they come into view.
- **Body armor is never rolled away.** Helmet, gloves and boots each turn up with `SurvivorGearPieceChance`
  (0.8), which is what makes them look scavenged rather than uniformed, but at that chance one survivor in
  five had a bare torso and read as a missing mesh instead. The chest slot now ignores the chance and falls
  back to any set that has the piece; set `SurvivorGearAlwaysChest` false for the old behaviour. Only affects
  looks rolled from then on - anyone already wearing a look keeps it, and `sc gear <name> none` still strips.
- **Clothes by tier** (your picks, 2026-10-08), at the same NPC tier as the weapons. Vanilla sets are weighted, nothing
  ruled out: light ones (and Primitive) likelier early, heavy ones later (`GearSetTiers`, `GearTierWeights`), and
  armor quality is 1-2 on T1 NPCs, 2-4 on T2, 3-5 on T3 (`GearQualityByTier`). With LittleRedSonja's armor packs
  installed, 35% of survivors and raiders (`SurvivorOutfitShare`, `RaiderOutfitShare`) wear one of 20 **whole mod
  outfits** instead, when one fits their sex, side and tier (`Outfits`: each outfit's pieces, sex, side, tier, weight
  and mix group). An outfit's pieces swap only with another outfit of its group, never with vanilla pieces, which
  clip; the female-only outfits (Little Kitty, the Boom Girls, Hit Girl) never go on a man, Motorgang never on a woman,
  and such a piece is left off rather than shown on the wrong body. Only outfits whose pack is installed are used. Set
  bonuses are player-only. With only one tier-0 raider outfit (Coyote), early raiders in a mod outfit all wear that one.
- How: 0-XNPCCore builds and animates the body (its `EModelSdcsNpc` model and controller); this mod hands it the
  look before the first build (`Patches_Xnpc.cs`) and adds the few fixes it lacks for these classes
  (`Patches_Sdcs.cs`: the eyes, hit colliders shots can reach, the jump animation, the get-up, and freeing a
  despawned body's meshes). Strangers that were never a prospect keep their face only for the session.
- Their eyes track you like a player's. That needs one repair: the eye shader spins each eyeball about a pivot
  given in head-bone space, while the game re-roots every untagged skinned mesh of a non-player entity onto the
  pelvis - which slid the eyeballs out of the face. The mod tags the generated eye mesh the way vanilla's own
  eye meshes are tagged and roots it at the head.
- **They heal only with items.** The NPC framework heals all of its NPCs for free (30 health every 15 seconds
  below 70%, no item, and on this rig no animation either). That is refused for these bodies. Instead each
  one is stocked once with up to two first aid bandages and, now and then, a first aid kit - a survivor in the
  inventory you see when you open them, a raider in its bag (dropped in its backpack) - and uses one when at or below 60% health
  and not already healing, 12 seconds apart. They use it the way you do: the item goes into their hand, the
  player's own third-person bandage animation plays, and when it finishes the item's vanilla effects fire and
  their weapon goes back in their hand, so a bandage heals what a bandage heals. They cannot attack while
  bandaging; a knockdown interrupts it and hands the item back. Saved in the middle of it (its chunk unloading behind
  you, or the game), it comes back with the item back in its inventory and its weapon - its gun loaded as it was -
  in hand; killed in the middle of it, the item and the gun's rounds are in its backpack.
  Hand a survivor medical items and they will use those; loot a dead one and you get what they had left.
- Knocked down, they get up the way you do. The game picks the knockdown and get-up animations from a
  different animator layer for players than for NPCs, deciding by whether the entity is a player; these
  bodies run the player's animator without being players, so they asked for animations their layer does
  not have and skipped the get-up. One more thing had to go with it: the ragdoll keeps an NPC's position
  pinned to its pelvis until nearly the end, which on this rig lifted them several metres into the air as
  the get-up clip raised the pelvis. The mod marks their ragdoll as a player's and leaves them on the ground
  once the physics part is over (`XnpcBodyGetUp`; `XnpcBodyJumpAnim` and `XnpcBodyColliderLayers` switch the
  jump animation and the hit colliders the same way).
- Known limits: no per-weapon hand joints (the player rig has one weapon hand, which vanilla's third-person
  weapon offsets already handle), no dismemberment, other players in multiplayer would see them undressed
  (host/single player only, like the rest of this mod).

## The NPC's line and voiced dialogue (the voice test, 2026-10-16)

- **The line.** Vanilla 3.2's dialog window shows only your responses, and SCore shows an NPC's line as a subtitle only
  for its older body type, so the player-body survivors' lines never reached the screen. The NPC's current line now
  shows in a panel above the responses, for every NPC but SCore's older type (`DialogLine`; `windowScDialogLine` in the
  vanilla dialog group, `XUiC_ScDialogLine`).
- **Voices.** The community's survivors (recruits, radio contacts, members, stand-ins) speak a line when the mod has a
  recording of it: `Resources/Voices/<male|female>_<Name>/<line>.ogg`, mono Ogg Vorbis (`VoiceLines`, `ScVoice`).
  - Each survivor speaks in one voice of their sex, picked from their name, so they keep it while the voice list stays
    the same (a new voice of their sex may re-deal it).
  - A clip is named after the line's statement id (`scFavourPoi.ogg`), or `<dialog>.<statement>` for another mod's line
    (`SdcsNPC.start.ogg`, 0-XNPCCore's greeting). An enclave request's words, picked at runtime by kind and standing,
    are named after their Localization key (`scEncAsk_gun_friendly.ogg`). A line with a name or a number in it says
    only the part without them; the panel shows the whole line. A `.txt` beside each clip holds the words it says.
  - `python Tools/voice_lines.py list | check | sidecars` lists the voiced lines, checks every voice folder, and writes
    the `.txt` files.
- **Playback** goes through Voice Control's player on the NPC when Voice Control has set one up, as it does for
  survivors near you: its face lip sync, and its TTS volume and range from its `modconfig.xml`. Otherwise SC plays the
  clip itself, with no lip sync. A new line replaces whatever the NPC was saying, Voice Control's own speech included;
  closing the talk stops only SC's clip. The same line from the same survivor is not spoken again within 30 seconds (it
  still shows), so going back to a talk's first menu doesn't repeat the greeting.
- **The shipped voices**, three of each sex - `male_Vane`, `male_Oxley`, `male_David`, `female_Jessica`,
  `female_Beth`, `female_Mora` - each say all 147 lines: every survivor line in SC's talks, the enclaves' included,
  and 0-XNPCCore's greeting. They are Vane, B. Hardscrabble Oxley, David, Jessica, Beth and Mora from the ElevenLabs
  Voice Library, generated with ElevenLabs (eleven_v4), one take each - Vane's and Jessica's first seven lines on
  2026-10-16, the rest on 2026-10-17 (round vo2) - and converted to mono Ogg Vorbis. A new voice is a new folder with
  the same file names; `Tools/voice_lines.py check` shows what it still lacks.
- **Closing the talk** stops SC's clip, except an enclave leader's answer to "Let me think about it.": it comes as a
  tell as the talk closes, and is spoken then.
- **Voice Control keeps quiet during the talk (V1, `MuteVcInTalk`).** While you talk to one of the community's survivors,
  and while SC's line still plays on them, 0-XNPCCore's Voice Control does not speak on that survivor: no walk-up or
  follower greeting, say-line ("I'm out of ammo.") or order ack lands over SC's line, nothing it had queued before the
  talk plays over it (a queued sentence its own watchdog releases after the talk has closed can still play then, after
  SC's line), and its HUD subtitle is hidden as the talk opens (a sentence already playing then is cut by SC's line,
  or ends by itself when that line has no recording). Voice Control's calls still finish as if spoken, so it greets and
  chats normally once the talk is over, and SC never flushes its speech queue (that could block every NPC's greeting for
  the session). Speak to the survivor through Voice Control during the talk (push-to-talk, typed chat, or "What are you
  carrying?" on 3.4) and they answer through it until SC's next recorded line. Other NPCs, and SC's survivors outside a
  talk, are untouched. In multiplayer each machine holds its own talk: a line relayed from another player's machine is
  not played on yours while you talk to that survivor. `sc voice gate` shows whom Voice Control is held on and how many
  of its lines were kept quiet; `sc voice sayline [line] [seconds]` gives the nearest survivor one of 0-XNPCCore's
  say-lines through Voice Control after a delay (`ammo.dry`, 5 s), to try it with a talk open.
- **Console:** `sc voice` (the switches, the player in use, the voices, the voice gate), `sc voice play <line> [id|name]`,
  `sc voice stop`, `sc voice rescan`, which picks up new or replaced clips without a restart, `sc voice gate` and
  `sc voice sayline [line] [seconds]`.

## How it hangs together (for modders)

- NPCs are 0-XNPCCore's `EntityAliveSDXV4` survivors (SCore's legacy `EntityAliveSDX` still works for pack NPCs). A resident is SCore's *FarmHere* shape: `Leader`/`Owner` cvars
  cleared, `hired_<id>` removed from the player, leader cache evicted, `buffOrderGuard` with the guard
  position set to the post, `Persist=1`, static spawner source, plus the `SCResident` utility-AI package
  added at runtime (`Config/utilityai.xml`). A follower is a plain SCore hire; the order it holds (`CurrentOrder`) is
  read as a member state (`ScOrders`), never repaired unless it is one nothing gives a follower (None, Hire, Task).
- The recruit dialog is injected by xpath into **every** dialog that has a `Hire` response, so character
  packs join the pool without being listed. The custom operators (`ScCanRecruit`, `ScHasHome`, `ScFavour`,
  `ScFavourDone`, `ScFavourQuestOpen`, `ScHasFavourItems`, `ScFavourPoiCleared`, `ScFavourBrief`, `ScStartPoiQuest`,
  `ScStartSuppliesQuest`, `ScTakeFavourItems`, `ScJoin`)
  live in `SurvivorCommunity.dll`.
- NPC cvars (`sc_member`, `sc_owner`, `sc_cardio` ...) mirror the registry so XML can read them; a cvar of
  exactly 0 is never saved by the game, so "false" is always -1 or absent.
- Harmony targets: every one is listed in `ModApi.ExpectedTargets` and checked at startup (`patch verification:
  all 111 targets patched` with Voice Control loaded, 97 without (the 3.4 build: 126 and 108); a `MISSING PATCH` line names one that did not
  bind). Among them
  `PersistentPlayerList.ReadXML/Write(string)` (registry load/save), the SCore NPC classes' `SetDead` (deaths) and
  `PostInit` (re-pin the spawner source), `SleeperEventData.Update` (no POI re-arm for favour quests, outposts and
  enclaves), `SleeperVolume.UpdatePlayerTouched` (no sleepers in an enclave's building; an infested building's count,
  spawn points and special), `SleeperVolume.UpdateSpawn`, `Spawn` and `ClearedUpdate` (an infested room's spawn step,
  its special, and the clear check; the feral share rides the `EntityGroups.GetRandomFromGroupList` postfix; the
  zombie-cap fix rides UpdateSpawn and UpdatePlayerTouched),
  `TEFeatureStorage.OnBlockActivated` (an enclave's containers), `SpawnManagerBiomes.SpawnUpdate` and
  `World.GetRandomSpawnPositionInAreaMinMaxToPlayers` (no wild NPCs on an enclave's grounds, with the group-pick
  postfix), the two turrets'
  and the motion sensor's target filters and `EntityAliveSDXV4.HandleNavObject` (raiders), SCore's two NPC
  inventory packages (`NetPackageHarvestInventoryRequest` / `...Update`: a remote player's open window),
  `EntityAliveSDXV4.UpdateWeapon(ItemValue, bool)` (a gun put away keeps its rounds), and `EntityAliveSDXV4.Write` /
  `Read` (the gun in hand is saved after SCore's own data, as `SCM1` + the item, and put back when the NPC loads; an
  older SurvivorCommunity or SCore alone skips those bytes).
- Locked & Loaded is reached by name only (`ScMags.cs`: `ModManager.ModLoaded("LockedAndLoaded")`, then
  `REInvMags.NpcMagazines`' `Arm`, `Refill`, `Rounds`, `Record`, `Restore` and `Carries`, bound with their exact
  signatures once per game); there is no reference to its DLL. Missing or throwing, it is switched off for the game
  with one log line, and SC's NPCs use loose rounds.
